## 1. Why a bible, and not just a character list

A recurring cast is not decoration. For a beginning reader it is **free comprehensible input**: a face already known costs nothing to process, so the child's whole attention goes to the Arabic. A new name and a new face in every unit spends attention on bookkeeping that the language should be getting.

It is also the cheapest source of the thing early-level material usually lacks — **affection**. A child who has met رزان four times will read a fifth story about her differently from a story about a stranger. That is not sentiment; it is time-on-task.

The cost of a recurring cast is **continuity debt**, and §4 is how this line refuses to pay it.

## 2. The house figure style (SC-053, amending SC-034)

Adopted deliberately and in full. What it means in practice:

**Unit 2 exception (SC-113, 2026-10-10):** rich full-colour cartoon with dimensional shading and scene backgrounds, per `UNIT2_RICH_STYLE_AUTHORITY.md`. The table below remains the earlier house grammar, not the Unit 2 rendering target. Unit 1 retains its existing art. Unit 2 identities remain fixed: Karim crown curls / blue; Zayd flat cap / green; Maha high topknot / coral; Omar straight spikes / green; Razan two low side bundles / teal; Khadija two front plaits / coral; Rana bob / mustard; cat orange-white, triangular ears and curled tail. Karim's large satchel is brown; Zayd's small satchel is black. These markers are distinct within Unit 2. The object variants are intentional and must not be conflated in assignments.

| | rule |
|---|---|
| **Line** | One weight (3 units), uniform, no tapering, no variation for emphasis. It should read as a fine felt-tip, not a brush. |
| **Value** | ~~Two values only: black line on white. No greys, no tints, no accent colours anywhere in an illustration.~~ **Amended by SC-111 (2026-10-01): hybrid editorial — black line on white, with flat colour on clothing only** (teal #2F7D78 · ochre #D3A344 · coral #D97668). No greys, no tints, no colour on faces, props or backgrounds unless a later ruling says so. Backgrounds stay true white (SC-037). |
| **Head** | Large — roughly **one third** of total figure height. It sits directly on the body; there is no neck. |
| **Face** | Two solid dots for eyes (no whites, no iris) · a small hooked nose · **one line for the mouth**, which carries the entire expression · brows only when the beat needs them. |
| **Body** | Tube limbs, no articulated joints, rounded ends for hands and feet. Noodly, not skeletal. |
| **Identity** | **One fixed marker per character**, never varied — see §3. Identity is never carried by face shape, size, or clothing detail. |
| **Space** | Near-empty. A floor line, a doorframe, one chair. No perspective, no backgrounds, no crowd fill. |
| **Expression** | Eyebrow angle, mouth line, and whole-body posture. **Never** facial detail. |

**Two deliberate departures from the reference style, both for teaching reasons.** (1) Illustrations sit in **framed panels**, not floating in the text column — the six-picture strip is a sequencing task and needs discrete, countable frames. (2) **Generic demonstration figures carry no face** (the walking figure that means *enters*, *sees*, *says*). A face makes a figure a person; those are actions, and a face would invite a child to ask *who*.

**A practical consequence worth knowing** *(superseded in part by SC-111 — the frames now carry clothing colour, so the print proof must test four-colour and greyscale conversion)*: two-value interior art is **one-colour printing**, which is materially cheaper per signature than four-colour and survives photocopying — which is what actually happens to a school worksheet.

**What is borrowed and what is not.** The borrowed thing is the *grammar* — line weight, empty space, identity-by-marker, emotion-by-posture, the achievable-looking hand. The cast below is this course's own, and no figure in this line may be a recognisable version of a character from the reference work.

## 2b. The world is not the classroom (SC-087)

**رَزَان travels.** School, home, her own room, the market, the beach, a street, a shop — **any setting a story needs.** Nothing in the charter, the scope map or the blueprint ever confined this course's stories to a classroom; the charter says the course is *for* classrooms, which is where the book is used, not where its stories happen, and it says content is *"culturally warm and universal in its core."*

**And the cast list below is a resource, not a roster to fill.** A setting may contain **whoever the plot needs** — a shopkeeper, a neighbour, a younger brother, a stranger, a woman at a stall. Characters are **recruited by narrative need**, never selected because they are available.

**Three things this does not relax.**

**Marker permanence still binds.** Anyone who returns needs a permanent marker (§3's rule). A character who appears once needs only to be drawn consistently *within that story*, and is not added to the standing cast unless a later story calls them back.

**The reserved students stay reserved.** آدَم · طَارِق · نُور · بَدْر are on Unit 1's roll and belong to school stories. **They are not a quota to be worked through**, and a story set outside school has no obligation to use them.

**New settings cost vocabulary.** A market needs market nouns; a beach needs a beach. That load is charged against the unit's band like any other, and a setting is chosen because a story needs it — not for variety.

**Why this is recorded here rather than assumed.** The cast list is eleven children in one room plus a teacher and a grandmother, and SC-054 added four more children. **A population that is a class list produces stories that happen where the class is** — which is how Unit 2's first two drafts became two versions of *where is the picture*. The reader line has a grandmother who cannot read her son's letters, a woman who writes letters for strangers, a key on the ground, a man in the rain. **That is a world, and it is why those stories have stakes a mislaid photograph cannot reach.**

## 3. The standing cast

**Markers are permanent.** A character's marker is how a child recognises them; changing it in a later unit is a comprehension failure, not a design refresh. Markers may be added to (a prop for one scene) but never replaced.

### The reserved students (SC-054) — already on the roll, not yet met

**The class is larger than the children who have appeared.** Unit 1's functional text prints a roll of **eight**, and four of those names have never had a story: **آدَم · طَارِق · نُور · بَدْر**. Until SC-054 the build document called them "filler names". They are not filler. They are **reserved students** — real members of this class who have simply not been the subject of a story yet, and who may arrive in any later unit.

| name | gender | marker (reserved, permanent) | figure id | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **آدَم** | boy | freckles on a bare short head | `av-adam` | drawn · on the class picture |
| **نُور** | **girl — confirmed, SC-062** | a single plait over one shoulder | `av-nur` | drawn · on the class picture |
| **بَدْر** | boy | a side-swept dark fringe | `av-badr` | drawn · on the class picture |
| **طَارِق** | boy | bare head with prominent ears | `av-tariq` | drawn but **held off the class picture** — he is the gap |

**Why they are drawn now rather than when they first speak.** Unit 1's roster task asks the child to *find the name with no face in the class picture*. That task cannot exist without a class picture, and a class picture cannot exist without these faces. The task had been specified and the picture never made; SC-054 made it.

**طَارِق is the gap, and that is not arbitrary.** The story already names him and never shows him — «هُنَاكَ اسْمٌ: طَارِق!». Making him the missing face means the puzzle and the story **agree** instead of merely coexisting: the child who read the story has a reason to expect the answer, and the child who did not can still solve it by matching. A random absence would have taught nothing.

> **CLOSED — نُور is a girl (SC-062, 2026-09-13).** The name serves both genders; the owner ruled **girl**, on the two grounds recorded when the question was raised: it balances the roll at four and four, and the marker vocabulary (§2) settles gender on sight, so a plait decides it without a word. **هٰذَا/هٰذِهِ and صَدِيق/صَدِيقَة in every future story now have their answer**, and SC-036's same-gender rule can be applied to her without a prior question.

**The roll can grow.** The functional text's cap is ≤30 words and it currently spends twelve. There is room for roughly a dozen more names if you want a deeper reservoir before Unit 2 — say the word and they are added to the printed list, given markers here, and drawn.

## 4. The laws of recurrence

These exist so that a permanent cast never becomes a permanent obligation.

**R-1 · Characters carry markers and dispositions. They never carry plot history.** كريم loses things in every unit he appears in; كريم does **not** remember the pen. A disposition is reusable forever and costs a new reader nothing. A remembered event is a debt every later story has to service.

**R-2 · No story may require memory of another story.** A child may begin this course at any unit — a mid-year transfer, a repeated year, a school that starts at Unit 3. Every story stands alone. Recurrence is a **bonus for the child who has been there**, never a toll on the one who has not.

**R-3 · Class stories cast from the standing cast, and the class is bigger than the cast (amended by SC-054).** A story set in the school world uses these children in their fixed markers — and it may draw on the **reserved students** (§3b), who are already on the roll and free to arrive at any time. A reserved student's first story is an **ordinary introduction**, never a callback: the text does not say *you have seen this name before*, because for many readers it will not be true. The recognition is a private pleasure for the child who noticed, which is the whole design.

**Inventing a classmate who is not on the roll still requires a reason** — a role none of the existing children can play — and that character then enters §3 or §3b with a marker, checked against the reservation table, **before being drawn.**

**R-4 · Not everyone appears every time.** A story uses the two or three it needs. A crowd of seven on every page destroys the recognition value the cast exists to create, and makes every frame harder to read.

**R-5 · Recurring characters accumulate no relationship arcs.** This guards **SC-027** and **SC-036**: nothing that could read as romance, friendship claims same-gender only, يُحِبّ aimed at things and never at named persons. A standing cast across eight units is exactly the condition under which a drift of this kind could creep in unnoticed, so the guard is stated here as well as in the register.

**R-6 · The art brief for every new unit names its cast before a line is drawn**, and any new marker is added to §3 in the same pass. An illustration commissioned against a cast that is not written down here is out of process.

## 5. Standing QC

- **Marker check** — every figure in a rendered unit resolves to a `av-*` in §3, and no marker has changed from its recorded definition.
- **Two-value check** — no illustration contains a fill or stroke that is not the ink or white. (The Studio's own review interface is not an illustration and is out of scope; so is the printed page's furniture, which is a separate design decision.)
- **Faceless-generic check** — no generic demonstration figure has acquired a face, and no named character has lost one.
- **R-1 check** — a draft that refers to an event from an earlier unit returns to drafting.
- **Reservation check** — every marker in a rendered unit appears exactly once in the reservation table, and no new character was drawn before being entered here.
- **Roll check** — every name printed on a class roster resolves to a character in §3 or §3b. A name on the roll with no entry here is an unreserved student and a defect.
