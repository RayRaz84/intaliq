## 1 · Purpose and communicative affordance

Saying **what I have, what I don't have, and what I want** — for things and for people — and asking a friend the same. The learner leaves able to say `عِنْدِي ___` · `لَيْسَ عِنْدِي ___` · `أُرِيدُ ___` · `أَحْتَاجُ إِلَى ___`, to say who is in their family («هٰذَا أَبِي. هٰذِهِ أُمِّي.») and who has what («عِنْدَ أَخِي كُرَةٌ»), and to ask «مَاذَا تُرِيدُ؟» · «عِنْدَكَ ___؟».

**Headline structural event — one:** **possession completed as a system** — `عِنْدَ` and `لِـ` with the five singular attached pronouns, productive (`عِنْدِي · عِنْدَكَ · عِنْدَكِ · عِنْدَهُ · عِنْدَهَا` · `لِي · لَكَ · لَكِ · لَهُ · لَهَا`), and its negation `لَيْسَ عِنْدِي / لَيْسَ لَهُ` with the agreement rule taught right the first time. Unit 1 debuted the affirmative chunks (SC-016) and Unit 2 exposed the suffixes input-first (SC-090); **Unit 3 finishes what Unit 1 started** (scope map). `أُرِيدُ` + noun rides along as the want frame it already is.

*Classification read off the grammar:* `عِنْدَ` is an adverb of place used possessively as a fronted predicate (Alhawary 4.2.1.4 p. 93, ex. 41 `عِنْدَهُ`; 8.3 p. 148 "words used as adverbs of time or place"); `لِـ` is the preposition of possession (6.1.9 p. 124); the attached pronouns are 5.3 p. 108 (eye-confirmed 2026-09-26); `لَيْسَ` negation of the nominal sentence is 4.2.2 p. 95 (eye-confirmed). The family nouns with a suffix (`أَبِي · أَخِي`) fall under the "five nouns" (14.10 p. 258 — **to verify by eye before freeze**); their case alternation is orthographic presence only (SC-062, SC-075).

## 14 · Story 1 — situation, engine, shape
**Situation (no Arabic):** A boy at home wants the ball; the ball is with his little sister, who wants his book; the book is with his mother, who wants her pen back; the pen is with his father, who needs nothing but the cat's cushion; the boy trades around the house, one exchange per person, until the ball reaches him — and it is the cat, sitting on the ball all along, who had it.
**Engine:** **C · Exchange** (new to the level; rule 4 satisfied). **Shape:** **Accumulation** — one exchange per family member, the pattern becoming visible, the last step breaking it. **Absurd element (CR-9):** the cat, who holds the ball and "wants" the father's cushion — the turn, one recycled support word (`قِطّ`). **Object-state table** required at drafting: the ball has one holder at each beat, and every `عِنْدَهُ / عِنْدَهَا` line is true when spoken.

## 15 · Story 2 — situation, engine, shape
**Situation (no Arabic):** رَزَان went to خَدِيجَة's house with one gift for her; at the door she met خَدِيجَة's small brother, who had nothing and wanted exactly that; she had one gift and two people — she gave it to the brother; خَدِيجَة, who saw, laughed and gave رَزَان something of her own — and the brother, who now had the gift, gave رَزَان his best thing, a stone.
**Engine:** **H · Choice / Dilemma** (new to the level). **Shape:** **Reversal** — the reader expects the gift to reach خَدِيجَة; it does not; what comes back is more than what went. **Absurd element:** the stone as the little brother's treasure — a support word if drafting wants it (`حَجَر`, lookup owed), or the gift returns to رَزَان by the cat's route; drafting decides and discloses. **Emotion by action, one told word at most (CR-8).** **Dignity (Standard 18):** the brother is small, not foolish; خَدِيجَة loses nothing.
