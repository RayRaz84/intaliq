# Unit 1 — Quality Gate, run against the Two-Story Blueprint

*Rule 28, run on both adopted v7 readings · 2026-09-13 · counts are machine-generated, not estimated · **this is the first time either reading has been assessed against these rules**, since both were adopted before the blueprint existed*

---

## Verdict first

**Story 1 «قَائِمَةُ رَزَان» — PASSES with one rule broken.**
**Story 2 «مَنْ أَنْتَ يَا قَلَم؟» — PASSES with two rules broken.**

Three defects, all fixable without redesigning either story. Two of them are in text adopted in the last forty-eight hours, which is the system working: the rules arrived and immediately caught things.

---

## 1 · Rule 19 — natural Arabic. **Story 1 FAILS.**

The rule names the exact pattern to avoid: *"relentless sequences such as آدم يريد… آدم يذهب… آدم يرى… آدم يقول…"*

Story 1 has **19 verb-plus-named-subject narration clauses in 138 words**, and they are dominated by two:

| clause | count |
|---|---|
| **تَقُولُ رَزَان** | **6** |
| **تَرَى رَزَان** | **4** |
| يَقُولُ عُمَر | 2 |
| تَدْخُلُ رَزَان · تَقُولُ هُدَى · يَرَى عُمَر | 1 each |

**Ten of nineteen narration clauses are [verb] + رَزَان.** That is the rule's own example with the names swapped.

Rule 19 also supplies the repair, and none of it costs vocabulary: **pronoun continuation** (a second clause about رزان does not re-name her), **natural subject omission**, and **verb-first clauses without the explicit subject**. The story already does this correctly once — «تَرَى رَزَان وَلَدًا. **تَقُولُ**: «أَنْتَ عُمَر!»» drops the subject on the second verb. The fix is to do that consistently instead of once.

**Editor's note on why this happened.** The unit's encounter floors reward printing تَقُولُ and تَرَى with a visible subject, because that is what makes them countable. Rule 19 says the opposite: count fewer, sound better. **Where the two conflict, the blueprint now governs** — and this is the clearest early case of it.

## 2 · Rule 12 — variation inside repetition. **Story 2 FAILS.**

The three refusals are **the identical sentence, three times**:

> «لَا! لَيْسَ عِنْدِي قَلَمٌ!» — مَهَا
> «لَا! لَيْسَ عِنْدِي قَلَمٌ!» — زَيْد
> «لَا! لَيْسَ عِنْدِي قَلَمٌ!» — فَرِيدَة

Rule 11 licenses the *situation* — *"a character must ask more than one person"* is on its list of motivated repetitions. Rule 12 refuses the *execution*: **"repetition should not mean copying the same sentence… the semantic core repeats while discourse changes."**

The blueprint's own worked example is exactly this shape and shows the remedy — three speakers, one meaning, three different surfaces. Here that could be a refusal, a refusal plus a reason, and a refusal plus a redirection, all inside the licensed envelope. Rule 15 also notes that **repeated failed attempts** are a source of beginner humour, which identical sentences flatten.

## 3 · Rule 14 — emotional vocabulary. **Story 2 FAILS, and this confirms the flag raised at adoption.**

«**كَرِيم حَزِين.**» names the emotion. Rule 14: *"Instead of repeatedly writing هو حزين / هو سعيد, the story may show the reason."*

The rule's escape clause does not apply: high-frequency emotional adjectives may be recycled **when they are target language**, and حَزِين was admitted as COMPREHENSIBLE_SUPPORT, not as a target.

This was flagged as a craft risk when the line was adopted under SC-056; the blueprint has now settled it. The sentence is also doing arithmetic work — it carries the **لَيْسَ** encounter that repaired the floor — so removing حَزِين must keep «لَيْسَ عِنْدَهُ قَلَمٌ.», which is the half that shows rather than tells.

---

## Everything else passes

**Comprehension** — 23-item core, three support items, one recognition-only. Core dominates both texts.

**CI/TPRS · dialogue (rules 8–10).** Both readings are close to half dialogue — **story 1: 67 of 138 words (49%) · story 2: 39 of 69 (57%)** — and the dialogue supplies what narration structurally cannot:

| | first person | second person |
|---|---|---|
| across both readings | أَنَا 11 · لِي 4 · عِنْدِي 3 · اسْمِي 1 · أُرِيدُ 1 | أَنْتَ 2 · أَنْتِ 2 |

*Second person is thin.* Rule 8 lists عِنْدَكَ / عِنْدَكِ / تُرِيدُ among what dialogue should naturally supply, and Unit 1 has **none of them** — every possession question is asked in the third person («مَنْ عِنْدَهُ قَلَمٌ؟») rather than to a person («عِنْدَكَ قَلَمٌ؟»). Not a failure at Unit 1, where أَنْتَ/أَنْتِ are themselves new, but it is the obvious growth point for Unit 2 and is recorded as such.

**Narrative (rule 3).** Both survive the test — *if the learner understood every word, would there still be a reason to continue?* Story 1: which of these children is which, and will she end up with anyone. Story 2: who has the pen, and then the reveal that it answers for itself.

**Rule 9 — does the dialogue do story work?** Yes in both, and it is worth naming: هدى's «لَيْسَ اسْمِي سَارَة!» *corrects a misunderstanding* and is the story's turning point; عمر's «هُنَاكَ اسْمٌ: طَارِق!» *gives a clue*; the pen's «أَنَا قَلَمُ كَرِيم!» *causes the discovery*. Remove any of the three and the story changes. That is the rule's own test, passed.

**Rule 13 — anchors.** Story 1's list matters because **رزان's own name is on it** — not because قَائِمَة is a unit noun. Story 2's pen matters because **it is his** and it answers. Both specific.

**Rule 17 — information control.** Character knowledge is grounded throughout: رزان guesses from the list, هدى corrects from her own name, سارة reads the list herself. **One item to watch:** the art brief has the pen visible behind كريم's ear in all three frames while he searches. That is dramatic irony rather than a leak — the reader is meant to be ahead of him, which is rule 15's *"visual contradiction"* humour — but it sits close to rule 17's line and should be a deliberate decision, not an accident.

**Rule 18 — possession traceable.** Nothing changes hands in either story. Clean.

**Rule 20 — tense honesty.** Story 2 narrates in the past with authorised forms only (دَخَلَ · قَالَ/قَالَتْ · رَأَى) and keeps present forms inside dialogue, which is precisely the pattern rule 7 asks for.

**Rule 22 — the two stories differ.** Required: three dimensions. Achieved: **protagonist** (رزان / كريم) · **engine** (Mistaken Identity / Search + Failed Attempts) · **problem** (who is who / where is it) · **object** (list / pen) · **ending** (belonging / absurdity) · **interaction type** (greeting and claiming / asking and refusing). **Six.**

**Rule 25 — length.** 138 and 69. Unequal by design, which the rule permits explicitly.

---

## What this changes about the outstanding decisions

The **لِـ family floor** (5, under 6) was going to be closed by giving سارة the signature frame. That repair is now **also a rule-19 improvement** — «لِي صَدِيقَةٌ: رَزَان!» is a different surface from «رَزَان صَدِيقَتِي!», which is what rule 12 asks for, and it removes nothing. The case for it strengthened.

The **six-versus-seven frames** question is untouched by the blueprint, except that rule 17 makes the art's relationship to withheld information an explicit standard.

# Part 2 — the Vocabulary Audit (V22), run 2026-09-13

*Added under SC-058, when the Vocabulary Recycling Architecture was adopted. Counts machine-generated.*

## Verdict

**Story 1 — PASSES.** **Story 2 — PASSES V15, STRAINS V4.** And the pair **cannot receive final approval yet**, for a reason that is about the curriculum rather than either story.

## V3 — does Story 1 carry the main lexical introduction? **Yes.**

Story 1 carries **17 of the unit's 21 new core items**; Story 2 carries 4. That is the distribution V3 asks for, and it happened without the rule existing. Story 1's support load is 3 items against 17 new core — V3's *"do not surround several new structures with several new nouns"* is satisfied.

## V15 — does Story 2 recycle Story 1? **Yes, comfortably.**

**9 of Story 2's 15 items (60%) are shared with Story 1**: السَّلَام · قَلَم · عِنْدَ · مَنْ · هُنَاكَ · أَنَا · صَدِيق · لَيْسَ · لِـ. The learner meets a new plot built mostly from words they met two days earlier, which is exactly *"I know most of these words"* rather than *"I already read this story."*

## V4 — is Story 2 lexically lighter than Story 1? **Strained, and the strain has a date on it.**

Story 2 introduces **six items Story 1 does not have**: دَخَلَ · قَالَ · رَأَى (the past-tense system) plus **أُرِيدُ · حَزِين · يَتَكَلَّمُ**.

V4's prohibition is specifically about simultaneity: *"Do not make Story 2 simultaneously introduce: a new tense system; many new nouns; many new verbs; a complex new plot."* Story 2 introduces the tense system **and** three further lexical items in 69 words.

**Before 2026-09-13 this rule was clean.** Story 2 carried the past forms and nothing else new. The three additions that strain it — أُرِيدُ, حَزِين, يَتَكَلَّمُ — arrived in the owner's revision under SC-056, a day before the rule existed, and each of them **improved the story**: أُرِيدُ gave it the stated want the blueprint requires, يَتَكَلَّمُ gave it the punchline it lacked, حَزِين carried the لَيْسَ floor repair.

So this is not a bad revision meeting a good rule. It is **two good things in tension**, which the architecture's appendix now names as the expected recurring conflict. The document also supplies the resolution:

> **Introduce the word in Story 1, where V3 says new core belongs, and let Story 2 recycle it.** That satisfies V3, V4, V5 and V15 at once.

**Concretely, for أُرِيدُ:** Story 1 currently has an *implicit* want — «رَزَان لَيْسَ لَهَا صَدِيقٌ» implies she wants one and never says so, and blueprint rule 3 wants the want visible. If رزان states it, أُرِيدُ becomes Story 1 core, Story 2 recycles it instead of introducing it, and **three separate findings close together**: V4's strain, V5's thin exposure, and blueprint rule 3's implicit want. **Offered, not taken — it is a change to the owner's own text.**

## V5 / V12 — is exposure distributed, and do high-value words get more? **One serious imbalance.**

Every new core item appears in at least two channels. But the spread is wrong at both ends:

| | print encounters | platform activities |
|---|---|---|
| **أُرِيدُ** *(new core)* | **1** | **2** |
| **لَهَا** *(new core)* | 1 | 2 |
| **قَلَم** *(support)* | **12** | 2 |
| يَقُول *(core)* | 18 | 12 |

**A support noun has twelve print encounters while the unit's most immediately usable verb has one.** قَلَم's frequency is narratively earned — it is Story 2's anchor — so this is not a defect in the writing. It is a flag on the planning, and it is exactly what **V12** warns against: *do not allow an incidental support noun to displace a high-frequency core word from future input.*

## V6 — is delayed recycling planned? **It was not. It is now.**

Every U1 item's recycling was immediate — same story, same unit. The **Cumulative Recycling Map** opened today records the first intended delays. They are intentions, not built material.

## V16 — support classification. **Four decisions open, none taken.**

قَائِمَة · فَصْل · قَلَم · حَزِين all sit as support with no forward decision. V16 forbids silent promotion, so the Editor has recommended and not acted: **فَصْل** and **قَلَم** look like *promote later* (a classroom is where every story in this course happens; twelve encounters is already core-level exposure), **حَزِين** looks like *remain support* pending the rule-14 decision, **قَائِمَة** is genuinely open.

| | condition | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | current unit's important vocabulary gets sufficient meaningful exposure | ✗ — **أُرِيدُ and لَهَا do not** |
| 2 | Story 2 substantially recycles rather than replaces Story 1 | ✓ 60% |

| 4 | some older vocabulary deliberately retrieved | n/a — first unit |

**Two conditions were unmet at the start of this audit. One is closed by opening the map. The other — condition 1 — is closed by the أُرِيدُ move above, if the owner takes it.**

**Status: Unit 1's story pair is one decision away from final approval on vocabulary grounds.**

## The two residues, stated plainly

**Rule 19.** Three redundant namings were removed — «تَدْخُلُ رَزَان» one clause after «اسْمُهَا رَزَان», and two «تَقُولُ رَزَان» where she was already the subject. The **nine that remain each follow a different speaker** in a text that is 49% dialogue. Dropping the name there would leave a Novice genuinely unsure who is talking, and Arabic supplies no other marker in this construction. Further reduction is a **story-structure** change — fewer speaker alternations — not a line edit. Recorded as improved, not claimed as closed.

**أُرِيدُ.** Now in both readings: **2 print encounters, 4 activities**, up from 1 and 2. LC-6 wants ≥3 in the debut reading and ≥8 counting activities. One line closes it, and rule 11 licenses exactly its shape — *an expectation that keeps failing*: after «أَهْلًا يَا هُدَى!», **«أُرِيدُ صَدِيقًا...»**. Offered, not taken.

**لَهَا** is the other thin item — 1 print, 2 activities — and is owed a return in Unit 2 rather than a repair here.

## One observation worth keeping

The move that fixed V4 — putting أُرِيدُ in Story 1 — **also closed V3, V5, V15 and blueprint rule 3.** Five rules, one sentence. That is the shape the vocabulary architecture's appendix predicted, and it is worth remembering the next time Story 2 wants a word it does not have: **the answer is usually to give the word to Story 1.**

## Status

**Unit 1's story pair passes the narrative gate and the vocabulary gate**, with two named residues and a one-line option outstanding. Everything else blocking the unit is production — audio, photographs, typeset layout, the art — not writing.
