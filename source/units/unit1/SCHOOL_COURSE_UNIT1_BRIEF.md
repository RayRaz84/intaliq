# Unit 1 Design Brief — «مَنْ أَنْتَ؟» (v1, GATE A PROPOSAL)

*The School Course, Level 1, Unit 1 · status: PROPOSED — nothing below is approved until the owner's Gate A rulings (§9) · governed by: the charter, the Design Description, the working map v3, LC-1–LC-5, the Activity Architecture, the deferred-constructions list, the reading-generation prompt, `STORY_CRAFT_CONDITIONS.md`*

---

## 1. The unit in one line

**Affordance:** greeting people, introducing yourself, finding out who someone is — and saying what you have. **Headline grammar:** the equational (verbless) sentence as a working system — pronouns, demonstratives, and عِنْد-possession chunks as predicates (SC-016: "I have" without a verb keeps the unit true to its verbless identity while adding real communicative reach). **The learner leaves able to say who they are, ask who you are, point out who everyone else is, and say what they have — in print and in the room.**

## 2. Proposed core (LC-1) — **22 items as amended by SC-023** (scoped band exception, carried by the in-class input station)

*SC-023 additions to the v1 table below: **هُنَاكَ** (existential; the census opener stays banned as discourse) · **عِنْدَهُ/عِنْدَهَا + لَيْسَ عِنْدَهُ/عِنْدَهَا** (third-person possession and negation, chunks, agreement checked per instance) · verbs **يَقُول، يَرَى، يَدْخُل، يُحِبّ** (3sg present, m/f pair = 1). The definite article الـ is admitted as grammar glue. Original 16 below unchanged.*

**Chunk policy proposed (R-7):** possessive suffixes are **not** taught as a system at Level 1; they exist only inside the listed chunks (اِسْمِي، اِسْمُكَ، صَدِيقِي، صَدِيقَتِي) per Design Description §14. Pattern-noticing only, nothing productive beyond the chunks.

**Deferred-list check (SC-010): performed.** No plurals, no duals, no إضافة beyond the chunk forms, no relative clauses, no verbs at all — the unit is deliberately verbless, which is also its grammar lesson.

## 3. Recycling obligations (LC-3)

Trivially none — there is no prior unit. Recorded obligation instead: **every U1 core item is a standing recycling obligation on U2 and U3**, and the greeting/farewell chunks enter the every-lesson ritual permanently (charter's opening ritual), giving them lesson-frequency exposure for the whole level.

## 4. The main story — «قَائِمَةُ لِينَا» (Lina's List)

**Cast (substring check performed — R-4):** لِينَا (new girl, protagonist) · عُمَر and سَارَة (classmates) · the teacher (referred to as المُعَلِّمَة هُدَى). No name is a substring of another or of any inventory word. *(The check rejected an earlier candidate, رِيم, contained in مَرْيَم-class names — the rule works.)*

**Craft audit (the conditions, applied):**

- **Want, small and sayable:** لينا is new, and she wants to know *who is who* — day one, a room of strangers. Sayable entirely in the envelope: مَنْ هٰذَا؟ مَنْ هٰذِهِ؟
- **One concrete object, answer turned away:** the class list in her hand — names she can read, faces she cannot match. (The object doubles as the unit's functional text, and gives عِنْدِي its debut line: «عِنْدِي قَائِمَة».)
- **Stakes are dignity:** being the lost one on day one. The comedy of her wrong guesses never laughs *at* her — each wrong guess is confidently corrected by a smiling classmate, and every correction is one more introduction.
- **Motivated repetition:** the engine is guessing name after name — هٰذَا عُمَر؟ لَا! أَنَا سَارَة! — the equational sentence and the pronouns recycled by the plot itself.
- **Discovery late:** at the list's end, one name has no face left to match. سارة reads it aloud: لِينَا. It is her own — she was on the list all along. She is not a visitor; she is the class.
- **Resolution + one resonance:** the last line is سارة's, not the narrator's: «هٰذِهِ لِينَا. لِينَا صَدِيقَتِي.» The unspent string: the empty chair beside سارة, seen in the art in scene one, occupied in the final panel — never mentioned in the text.
- **Length (LC-6 — derived, not chosen):** the story debuts ~9–10 of the unit's 16 items; at ≥3 encounters each, that is ~30 new-token occurrences, which sets the floor. Target **80–110 words** under the ~120 ceiling — long enough to teach its load, no longer. Encounter counts are verified mechanically at QC; the ≥6-per-unit floor is completed by the parallel story and activities.

**Generation:** drafted via `READING_GENERATION_PROMPT.md` with this unit's §2 core as the inventory slot; the prompt's banned-features list and self-run checklist apply in full.

## 5. Parallel story — sketch

Same structures, four dimensions changed (context, protagonist, relationship, payoff): **a grandmother meets her grandson's friends at a birthday party** and mixes them up magnificently; the grandson corrects her; the final mix-up is deliberate — she calls the shyest guest صَدِيقِي, and means it. New setting (home), adult protagonist, warmth payoff instead of belonging payoff. Debuts 0–2 items → 40–80 words per LC-6.

## 6. Functional text + authentic corner

**Functional text:** قَائِمَةُ الصَّفِّ — the class roster itself, printed as a real list. S12 task: mark who is present in the story's final scene; find your own classroom's twin of each name. **S13 (Real Arabic Around You):** photographed name signs — a shop sign, a name plate, a school door — task: find the name; is it a person or a place?

## 7. Activities (Architecture applied — 8 sets, within cap)

Fixed core S1–S6, rotation **S8 (Who Says It?)** — earned, because dialogue carries the plot — and **S12 (Use the Text)**. Teacher engine: T1 over the classroom scene; T2 tells لينا's story before it is read; **T3 Live Search mirrors the engine: name cards distributed, learners match cards to classmates** — the story played for real; T4 rebuilds the story from panels. S5 (Ask Your Friends) grid: مَنْ أَنْتَ؟ with names collected — the learner ends the unit holding a list of real names they gathered themselves, the story's object made personal. S6 (My Turn): a name card / self-poster — هٰذَا أَنَا. أَنَا [name]. — displayed on the classroom wall.

## 8. The grammar pop-up (LC-4 — one, after the main story)

One box, four lines, examples from the story only: Arabic says it **without "is"** — أَنَا لِينَا. هٰذَا عُمَر. — and the pointing word matches the person: هٰذَا for عمر, هٰذِهِ for سارة. No terms, no table. عِنْدِي rides along as a chunk with no pop-up of its own — one pop-up per headline, and the headline is the verbless sentence it exemplifies. **R-3 asks the owner to rule on the pop-up's language** (proposal: English, as the single sanctioned English element in the student book — a notice a 9-year-old can actually read; everything else stays Arabic).
