**The tense split, as printed.** Narration is past — دَخَلَ · قَالَ · قَالَتْ · رَأَى. **Dialogue is present** — «مَنْ عِنْدَهُ قَلَمٌ؟» · «أَنَا قَلَمٌ!» · «هُنَاكَ قَلَمٌ!» — because that is what people say out loud. This is standard narrative practice and it is also what keeps the law affordable: **كَانَ and لَمْ يَكُنْ are not needed anywhere**, so past-tense possession never has to be said and no jussive enters Unit 1. Where v4 *narrated* the state («لَيْسَ عِنْدَهُ قَلَمٌ»), v6 puts it in the three refusers' **mouths** («لَيْسَ عِنْدِي قَلَمٌ!») — which is why the encounter repair of SC-047 cost the tense law nothing.

**The absurdity, and why it lands here.** The unit is «مَنْ أَنْتَ؟». Its second reading now ends with an **object answering that question** — «مَنْ... مَنْ أَنْتَ؟» «أَنَا قَلَمُ كَرِيم!» The joke is the unit's own title turned on a pen, and it costs **no new vocabulary at all**: the pen introduces itself with exactly the sentence pattern the learner has been practising all unit. Three honest refusals precede it (مها, زيد, فريدة — the owner's three-failures shape), the turn is impossible rather than merely surprising, and كريم is left perplexed while the pen is matter-of-fact — the reference shape recorded in Story Standard §E-3.

## 4. The grammar pop-up (English — R-3/SC-018; one box, after the main story)

> **Look — no "is"!**
> Arabic doesn't need a word for *is*: **أَنَا رَزَان** = I (am) Razanne. **هٰذَا عُمَر** = This (is) Omar.
> And the pointing word matches the person: **هٰذَا** for a boy or a man, **هٰذِهِ** for a girl or a woman.

*(Three lines. The only English in the book.)* ⟨**Narrowed 2026-10-10, §9 P1:** "In these present-tense sentences Arabic has no separate word for am / is …"; the pointing word "matches the person or thing it points at … and for قَائِمَةٌ, a 'she' word: هٰذِهِ قَائِمَةٌ".⟩

## 7. Art brief — the house figure style (SC-034, completed by SC-053)

> **The style and the cast now live in `SCHOOL_COURSE_CAST_BIBLE.md`, which is binding on every unit.** This section is Unit 1's *application* of it, and no longer the place where the rules are defined. Read the bible first.

**What SC-053 changed, in one paragraph.** The style is adopted in full: one stroke weight, **two values only — black line on white, no greys and no accent colours in any illustration**, heads at roughly a third of figure height sitting straight on the body, and **faces: two dots, a hooked nose, and one line for the mouth that carries the whole expression**. Expression variants are made only where a beat needs one. Two departures are deliberate: illustrations keep **framed panels** (the six-picture strip is a sequencing task and needs countable frames), and **generic demonstration figures stay faceless** (the walking figure means *enters*; it is an action, not a person). The cast is **permanent and recurring** under laws R-1…R-6 — most importantly **R-1: characters carry markers and dispositions, never plot history**, so no Unit 1 event may be referred to in a later unit.
