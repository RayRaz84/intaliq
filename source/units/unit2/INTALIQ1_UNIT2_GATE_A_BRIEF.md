## 1 · Purpose and communicative affordance

Asking and saying **where something is**. The learner leaves able to ask `أَيْنَ ___؟` and answer with a place, and to say that one thing is in, on or under another.

**Headline structural event — one:** **locative expressions** as a small set, plus the interrogative that calls for them.

*SC-082 classification, read off the grammar: `فِي` and `عَلَى` are **prepositions** (Alhawary 6.1.5 p. 122; 6.1.4 p. 121) · `تَحْتَ` and `هُنَا` are **adverbs of place** (8.2 p. 147) · `أَيْنَ` is an **interrogative locative** (7.1.8 p. 138). The label "locative prepositions" was wrong for three of the five.*

## 2 · ACTFL — observable performance first, label second

| Mode | What the learner does | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Interpretive reading | **Follows three successive statements about one object's location and identifies which of them locates it precisely enough to act on**, in vocalized text with picture support | **emerging Novice High** |
| Interpretive listening | Identifies which of two named locations a read-aloud sentence states | **Novice Mid** |
| Interpersonal | Asks `أَيْنَ ___؟` and answers with a licensed prepositional phrase naming a container or a surface | **Novice Mid** |
| Presentational | States where one object is, in one sentence, from a picture | **Novice Mid** |

**Revision 3 correction.** Revision 2 labelled the reading target Novice High on the strength of *"locates a stated place… with picture support"*, which is Novice Mid work. The label was not lowered; **the task was strengthened** — Story 1 now makes three successive claims about the same object, so the learner must hold a sequence and discriminate, which is what carries the emerging-Novice-High claim. **Performance, never proficiency.**

## 14 · Story 1 — engine, shape, object-state table

**Engine: a communication gap that only precision closes.** سَارَة is looking for the picture. رَزَان has it — it is in her own bag, under her book. Nothing is lost; what fails is the telling.

**Shape:** three attempts, each adding one increment of precision, resolved by the third.

### Object-state table

| Beat | Target object | Actual location | What رَزَان says | What سَارَة understands or does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `الصُّورَة` | in رَزَان's bag, under the book | «**هُنَا!**» | looks around the room — *"here"* names a place, not a container |
| 2 | `الصُّورَة` | **unchanged** | «**فِي حَقِيبَتِي.**» | opens the bag, sees `الْكِتَابُ` on top, does not see the picture |
| 3 | `الصُّورَة` | **unchanged** | «**تَحْتَ الْكِتَابِ.**» | lifts the book — finds it |

**The table proves what it must.** The picture has **one stable location** from beat 1 to beat 3 and never moves. **All three statements are simultaneously true** — it *is* here, it *is* in the bag, it *is* under the book. **Each attempt adds real precision**: place → container → position within the container. **The misunderstanding is plausible** because a deictic names no container and a container hides what is beneath its top item. **The resolution names only licensed language** — `تَحْتَ` · `الْكِتَابِ` · `حَقِيبَتِي`, all in §8.

## 15 · Story 2 — engine, shape, object-state table

**Engine: a false belief the text itself corrects.** The picture is on the door, and **the narration establishes that in the opening line**, so the reader knows. نُور believes it is in her bag. She checks, is wrong, checks again, is wrong, and finally sees it where it has been all along.

**Shape:** a confident claim, two disconfirmations, a question from بَدْر that forces her to say the belief aloud, and a resolution she reaches by looking up.

**Revision 3 removed the earlier design** in which نُور was holding the picture unknowingly. Two reasons, both the owner's: it risked making her look foolish, and **it made the illustration the sole source of a plot-essential fact**, which no image may be.

### The background rule (revision 3 — replaces the absolute ban)

> **No unnamed object may carry essential instructional or narrative meaning. Low-salience environmental details may appear where they are necessary to make a setting visually credible, but they must not compete with target objects, invite identification, create answer cues, or alter the story.**

In practice: a chair or a table edge may exist to make the room read as a classroom. **It may not be where anything is, be pointed at, be countable, or resemble a target object in silhouette.**

**The images must not add:** رَزَان's family, home or circumstances · any object carrying meaning the Arabic cannot name · text beyond what the story prints.

**The images must not reveal early:** **Story 1's frame 1 must not show where the picture is** — رَزَان knows, the reader does not, until she says it. **Story 2's frame 1 must show the picture on the door** — the narration says so in the same breath, so the image confirms a stated fact rather than supplying an unstated one. **The two stories have opposite disclosure rules and this remains the single most important instruction to the illustrator.**

**Scenes needing separate images because they are distinct causal steps:** Story 1 — **three**, one per attempt; collapsing them destroys the escalation. Story 2 — **four**: the establishing frame, the bag, the book, the door.
