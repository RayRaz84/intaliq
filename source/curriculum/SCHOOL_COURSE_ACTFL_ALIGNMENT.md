# The School Course — ACTFL Alignment Framework (v1, **ADOPTED** SC-048)

*The series' standing method for mapping levels to ACTFL, and the source from which each level's committee-facing alignment appendix is generated · status: **ADOPTED 2026-09-10 (SC-048)** — §§1, 4, 5 and 6 are binding, and §4's text-type audit is now a line in unit QC · reference editions: ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines (2024), ACTFL Performance Descriptors for Language Learners, NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements (2017) — editions are named so future revisions are a recorded update, not silent drift*

---

## 1. The four principles (binding)

**P-1 — The series claims performance, never proficiency.** The Proficiency Guidelines describe spontaneous, unrehearsed, domain-general ability; a curriculum produces performance — ability within the domains it taught. Every outward claim uses the performance formula:

> *"Learners completing Level N typically perform in the [range] (ACTFL) within the course's topic domains."*

No cover, catalog page, or appendix ever states or implies a proficiency guarantee.

**P-2 — Mapping runs through functions, never inventories.** Grammar and vocabulary lists map to nothing on the ACTFL scale; can-do statements map directly. Each unit's affordance is written as a can-do; a level's exit profile is the set of its can-dos; the set is located against the NCSSFL-ACTFL benchmarks and the Performance Descriptors. (The scope-and-sequence's affordance column is therefore the alignment's raw material — by design.)

**P-3 — The three modes are anchored separately.** Interpretive (reading/listening), interpersonal, and presentational each get their own anchor per level. The course's input-first method guarantees interpretive runs ahead of production; the asymmetry is stated openly as a design feature, never averaged into one number.

**P-4 — Claims carry evidence and stay revisable.** Each mapping is recorded with what supports it (benchmark citations, text-type audit, panel calibration, and — once available — learner work). When real learners are measured, the empirical result supersedes the estimate, on the record. This is the course's empirical-entry-profile doctrine applied to alignment.

## 2. Series orientation map (reservations, not specs — SC-008 applies)

Spacing on the ACTFL scale is uneven: the Novice→Intermediate crossing (memorized and recombined chunks → *created*, sentence-level language) is the major threshold, and the Intermediate plateau is long. The ladder is paced around those facts. Everything below Level 1 is orientation only, revisable when each level's predecessor has real exit data.

| Level | Orientation range | The level's functional headline |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Novice Low → Novice High** (SC-010, fixed) | Understand and use familiar words, chunks, and practiced sentences in the course's domains; first owned past forms at exit |
| 2 | Novice High → Intermediate Low | **The crossing:** consistent creation of original sentence-level language |
| 3 | Intermediate Low → Mid | Sustained simple conversation; connected sentences; past narration broadens |
| 4 | Intermediate Mid (interpretive toward High) | Paragraph-level discourse emerges; functional text range widens |
| 5 | Intermediate High (interpretive toward Advanced Low) | Narration across time frames consolidates; reduced-vocalization reading |
| 6 | Intermediate High / Advanced Low interpretive | Authentic-adjacent texts; presentational Intermediate Mid–High |

## 3. Level 1 exit profile as can-do statements (per mode)

The scope-and-sequence's exit profile, converted per P-2. Domains: self and identity, classroom and home, family, possessions, food and preferences, daily routine, school world, simple past events.

**Interpretive reading — anchor: Novice High (supported text).**
I can understand short, fully vocalized stories built from familiar language, following who, what, where, and why. I can understand simple functional texts — labels, a class roster, a menu, a schedule, a short note — on familiar topics. I can find the information I need in a short familiar-topic text.

**Interpretive listening — anchor: Novice Mid–High (visually supported).**
I can follow a told story on familiar topics when supported by pictures, gesture, and known language. I can understand questions and instructions built from familiar language.

**Interpersonal — anchor: Novice Mid → High.**
I can greet, introduce myself, and ask who someone is. I can ask and answer questions about where things are, what people have and want, and what people like. I can ask and answer questions about daily routines. At exit: I can ask and answer simple questions about what happened, using learned past forms.

**Presentational (speaking and writing) — anchor: Novice Mid.**
I can present myself, my family, and my likes with practiced sentences. I can describe my day. I can write captions, lists, labels, and short notes from familiar language. At exit: I can retell a familiar story's events in simple sentences with support.

*Benchmarking note of record: "recount a simple completed event with learned forms, on familiar content, with support" is Novice High performance — not Intermediate, which requires sustained creation of original language. Level 1's anchor is therefore honest as stated.*

## 4. The text-type audit (mechanical, per reading)

ACTFL bands correspond to text types, so interpretive claims are auditable against the texts themselves. The rule: **every Level 1 reading must match the Novice text profile** — short connected texts of simple sentences on familiar topics, high-frequency vocabulary, strong visual support, one clear event line. A reading whose discourse demands exceed the profile (dense paragraphs, embedded clauses, long referential distance) is flagged at QC *regardless of its vocabulary being licensed* — vocabulary compliance does not certify discourse compliance. This line joins the unit QC checklist so it cannot be lost behind a green ledger check.

## 5. Panel calibration (the verification model, SC-009, applied to alignment)

1. Panel members work from the Performance Descriptors, not from impressions of "level."
2. **Materials calibration** (per level, before release): the panel rates sample readings and activities against the claimed band per mode; disagreements are discussed to consensus or recorded as dissent.
3. **Learner-work calibration** (once the level has students): for a sample of learners, three pieces per mode are rated against the claimed range; the observed range replaces the estimated one in all claims, on the record.
4. Every calibration outcome is a register entry; outward copy is updated in the same pass.
5. ⟨SC-096⟩ The materials sample per level is drawn **per ladder rung** (`SCHOOL_COURSE_OUTPUT_LADDER_SCHEDULE.md` §4): Captions and Frames work is rated against Novice Low–Mid, Chains against Novice Mid–High, the Stories rung against Novice High — the level's exit claim is tested there.

## 6. Standing disclosures (appear in every alignment appendix)

- The course teaches **Modern Standard Arabic**; interpersonal ratings describe MSA interaction. (ACTFL Arabic testing accepts MSA and/or dialect; the course's scope is stated, not hidden.)
- Level 1–2 interpretive claims describe **fully vocalized text**. Ability with unvocalized text is a separate claim, made only at the levels whose specs schedule vocalization tapering.
- All claims are performance-within-domains per P-1, calibrated per §5, and dated.

## 6b. The two audiences for the same can-dos (SC-049)

A can-do exists in two forms, and the difference is not translation. **The committee sees it named** — *"I can introduce myself"* — in the English appendix of §7. **The learner sees it performed** — the actual sentence, «أَنَا ___», with a tick box, on the unit's last page. At Novice Low the learner cannot read a can-do statement in Arabic (أَسْتَطِيعُ أَنْ… is far outside the envelope) and English is unavailable in the student book beyond the single grammar pop-up, so **the student-facing page shows the function instead of naming it**. Ticking the sentence *is* the claim. This keeps P-2 intact — the mapping still runs through functions — while keeping the student book monolingual. Every unit's last page is built this way; the appendix is generated from the same list, in the other form.

## 7. Generating a level's committee-facing appendix

Each level's alignment appendix is assembled from this framework, never written fresh: the P-1 claim sentence with the level's calibrated range → the §3-style per-mode can-do list → the §6 disclosures → a one-paragraph description of the §4–§5 evidence behind the claims. One page, two at most. It changes no lesson; it answers the committee.
