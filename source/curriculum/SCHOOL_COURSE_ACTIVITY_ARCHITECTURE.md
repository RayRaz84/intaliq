# The School Course — Activity Architecture (v2.1, per SC-024; P-8 and platform rule 15 added per SC-038)

*The closed catalog of activity formats and the five-phase unit arc · v2 supersedes v1: input is student-independent (platform + textbook), output lives in the notebook, communication is the classroom's job, and nothing requires making props · **status: ADOPTED 2026-09-10 (SC-048)** · method authority: `SCHOOL_COURSE_DESIGN_DESCRIPTION.md`; constraints: LC-1–LC-6, SC-022–SC-024, charter §4*

---

## 1. Laws

**P-1 — The catalog is closed.** Every activity is an instance of a format below; new formats enter only by register ruling. Ritual beats novelty: a format seen eight times runs itself.

**P-2 — The communication test.** Completing any activity perfectly must tell someone something — about the story, the learner, or a classmate. Display-of-form activities do not exist.

**P-3 — Input, reading and writing are independent (SC-024/025; precised SC-119).** Every platform, textbook, and notebook format is completable by a student alone: the platform speaks, the textbook shows, the notebook frames. The test, applied to every proposed activity: *could a student alone in a room finish this with only the products in front of them?* An activity that needs a teacher's voice, hands, or judgment to complete belongs to phase 5 or does not exist. (v1's S1 listen-and-point failed this test and was removed — its listening job lives on the platform.) ⟨**SC-119**⟩ The one exception is named, not hidden: **scaffolded interaction (C1, phase 3) needs classmates** — it is rehearsed from printed language and asked of real people, and its answers feed the notebook; the independence law therefore reads *phases 1, 2 and 4 alone; phase 3's survey with the class; phase 5 with the class*. A learner absent for the survey has a printed alternative on the page it feeds (N2): the parallel story is written about the printed class list instead of the collected names.

**P-4 — Zero prep, zero props (SC-024).** No format may require the teacher or student to make, cut, print, or gather anything. All materials are the platform, the textbook, the notebook, and the people in the room.

**P-5 — One home per format.** Platform · textbook · notebook · classroom — each format lives in exactly one. Duplication across products is a QC defect.

**P-6 — Instructions are rituals.** Fixed icon + fixed short Arabic rubric per format, identical every unit. The single sanctioned English element remains the grammar pop-up (SC-018).

**P-8 — Vocabulary is met in a phrase (SC-038).** Wherever an activity can present an item inside a phrase rather than as an isolated word, it must; a bare word appears only where no phrase is possible. The chunk is the unit of acquisition, so a word shown alone is input the learner cannot use. Mechanically, every engine item carries a `phrase` and an `hl` (the target's exact substring inside it); QC asserts `hl` is verbatim in `phrase` and suppresses any item that fails. Print obeys the same law: labelled scenes, prompts, word banks and notebook frames carry phrases.

**P-7 — Content sensitivity (SC-027).** Anything that could remotely read as romance or a similar sensitive topic goes to the owner as a question before it is drafted — never resolved by the writer. Standing instance: يُحِبّ applies to things and activities, never to named persons, until the owner rules otherwise; no heart iconography.

## 2. The five phases (the unit arc)

| Phase | Where | Who runs it | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Input** | Platform (mainly) + textbook input pages | The student, alone | Meaning made: hear-and-tap words, verbs, possession; story preview |
| **2 · Reading** | Textbook | The student (class may read together) | The main story, then the parallel story; comprehension formats |
| **3 · Consolidation and scaffolded interaction** ⟨SC-119⟩ | Textbook, then the classroom | The student; then the class, teacher facilitating | Manipulation formats; the grammar pop-up; functional text; authentic corner; **then C1, the survey — rehearsed from the printed line, asked of real classmates — whose answers the notebook will use** |
| **4 · Output** | **The notebook** | The student, alone | The output ladder: guided retell → parallel story (from the survey's names) → own story → the share prepared |
| **5 · Fuller communication and transfer** ⟨SC-119⟩ | Classroom | Teacher facilitates, zero prep | C3 (show a partner, who asks), C4 (the retell), the cumulative personal questions, and the same language reused in a new small situation — using completed pages and the people present |

The order is law — meaning before reading; reading before output; **scaffolded interaction, including C1, before the notebook writing that depends on it; Phase 5 reserved for fuller communication and transfer** (owner's decision, carried forward from the 2026-10 source package and recorded as **SC-119**; SC-024's "output precedes interpersonal communication" is precised, not reversed: the *survey* is scaffolded interaction, not the fuller communication of phase 5). Pacing within it is free. A student who misses class completes phases 1, 2 and 4 alone; phase 3's survey needs the class (see P-3 for the absent learner).

## 3. The catalog

### A — Platform input formats (audio-first, tap/drag only, input-only; founded in `SCHOOL_COURSE_U1_STATION.md`)

**A1 · Faces and names** (hear a phrase, tap the person) · **A2 · Word garden** (hear a phrase, tap the thing) · **A3 · Verbs in motion** (hear a phrase, tap the animation) · **A4 · Who has it?** (hear عِنْدَهُ/لَيْسَ عِنْدَهُ, drag the object) · **A5 · Story preview** (watch scene 1 with audio, one tap-check) · **A6 · Likes** (hear يُحِبّ sentences and star-link a person to a THING — per SC-027, يُحِبّ never connects named persons at this level). No typing, no speaking into the device, no visible scores — a completion mark only.

**A9 · Personal questions · وَأَنْتَ؟ وَأَنْتِ؟** ⟨SC-091 · CR-16⟩ — each unit adds 8–15 second-person questions built from its readings' structures to a **cumulative** set asked in every unit's preview activities thereafter; input-tier (hear, tap, or answer from the phrase page), never scored. The course's standing substitute for the teacher's personalised questions (Blueprint 10, 23; SC-062 option (i)).

### B — Textbook input pages (the "some textbook" input; fully independent; also the no-device path)

**B1 · Picture spread · اُنْظُرْ!** — the unit's core words and verbs as one illustrated scene with **phrase labels** (SC-038: «هٰذَا وَلَدٌ», not «وَلَدٌ»); the print twin of A1–A4. **B2 · Preview panel · اُنْظُرْ إِلَى الْقِصَّةِ** — scene 1 as a full-page illustration with its lines, before the story proper; the print twin of A5.

**B0 · Phrase page · اُنْظُرْ وَاقْرَأْ!** ⟨SC-091 · CR-13⟩ — a numbered picture-phrase page listing **every** new core, support and input-first item the unit's readings print, as the phrase the story prints, with a gloss; 12–25 items typical, up to ~40 when a reading needs it; a reading aid — listing confers no assessable status. Precedes B1 and the strip.

### S — Textbook reading & consolidation formats (phases 2–3)

**S2 · اِقْرَأْ وَصِلْ** (read-match) · **S3 · صَحٌّ أَمْ خَطَأٌ؟ أَرِنِي** (judge + prove by pointing) · **S4 · اِخْتَرْ وَاكْتُبْ** (frames whose every completion is true) · **S7 · رَتِّبْ** (sequence panels) · **S8 · مَنْ يَقُولُ؟** (quote ↔ face) · **S9 · اِبْحَثْ فِي النَّصِّ** (text hunts) · **S12 · اِسْتَعْمِلِ النَّصَّ** (do something with the functional text) · **S13 · عَرَبِيَّةٌ حَوْلَنَا** (authentic print corner) · **S14 · الْآنَ أَمْ قَبْلُ؟** (sort lines into the present-tense first reading or the past-tense second reading; no rule stated, no grammatical term used — entered the closed catalog by register ruling SC-041, and exists in every unit because the tense law does). *(v1's S1 listen-and-point is absorbed by A1/A2 — listening lives on the platform, where the audio is.)* Per unit: 5–7 S-sets, within the light cap.

**S10 · Recombination page · اِقْرَأْ. ثُمَّ اقْرَأْ.** ⟨SC-091 · CR-14⟩ — 8–12 one- to three-sentence texts from the unit's phrase page and prior units, explicitly not stories, carrying the encounter floors (CR-2), free to use any character. **S11 · Version A / Version B · نَفْسُ الْقِصَّةِ... وَ** ⟨CR-15⟩ — Story 1's strip with one element changed (A: same cast, object moved) or setting and object changed (B), retold from pictures; plus the short-answer set carrying *if the answer is not in the story, invent it* with at least two unanswered questions. Retellings live here and never in the Story 2 slot (CR-11).

### N — Notebook output formats (phase 4; the output ladder, scaffolding fading across the level)

**Notebook art ⟨SC-100⟩:** the notebook prints **only pictures the student book has already printed** — N1 reprints the reading's strip, one frame per line at the Captions rung; N4 reprints earlier units' faces and objects from the same symbols; no picture is ever drawn for a notebook page. The learner's own drawings (اُرْسُمْ boxes on N2, N3, S6) are the learner's and are not governed. Every notebook page is audited at freeze for **picture–instruction fit**: each printed frame must be the frame its line describes, and each drawing prompt must name what the activity actually asks the learner to draw.

**The fade is scheduled ⟨SC-096⟩:** `SCHOOL_COURSE_OUTPUT_LADDER_SCHEDULE.md` §3 — Captions U1–U2 · Frames U3–U4 · Chains U5–U6 · Stories U7–U8; one scaffold removed per pair of units; past forms copied, never built, until U8. Each Gate A names its rung (template field 4c).

**N1 · Write the story · اُكْتُبِ الْقِصَّةَ + the story's name** (SC-043 — the rubric names the story it belongs to, so a unit's two retells are told apart by title, never by number) — the story's pictures reprinted in order with a **بَنْكُ الْكَلِمَاتِ** (word bank); the learner writes the line each panel needs (early units: copy-and-complete; later: write from the picture).
**N2 · My parallel story · قِصَّتِي الْمُوَازِيَةُ** — the story's frame with 3–4 elements swapped by the learner (names, object, place as the level allows); the notebook prints the skeleton, the learner makes it theirs.
**N3 · My own story · قِصَّتِي** — the freest tier, still framed at Level 1: the learner builds a story page about their real world from sentence starters and their own earlier pages (S4 truths, C1 survey data). By late Level 1 the frames thin.
**N4 · Do you remember? · هَلْ تَذْكُرُ؟** — delayed retrieval from *earlier* units only.

**S6 · دَوْرِي (My Turn) moves to the notebook** as the make-and-display piece feeding C3.

### C — Classroom communicative formats (C1 in phase 3, C3–C4 in phase 5 ⟨SC-119⟩; teacher facilitates; zero prep, prop-free)

**C1 · Ask your friends · اِسْأَلْ أَصْدِقَاءَكَ** — the survey, its grid printed in the notebook; the data collected is real names and answers. **Phase 3** (scaffolded interaction): it runs before the notebook page that uses its names. ⟨**SC-124**⟩ **Prepared before use:** the page prints each question with its answers (question → answer pairs), and the teacher first models every exchange — asking real learners the printed questions, with a real object where one fits — then the class rehearses each exchange in pairs, and only then does the survey begin. C1 is a classroom exchange that feeds N2; it is **not** a survey of learners about the book. Its printed questions address the classmate directly in the second person, with masculine and feminine forms side by side and the answer models printed, and every column has a question that elicits it (Unit 1 repaired 2026-10-10, audit B5).
**C2 · Half and half · نِصْفٌ وَنِصْفٌ** — paired information gap; the A and B halves are printed pages in the textbook (printed ≠ props; nothing is made).
**C3 · Share your page · هٰذِهِ صَفْحَتِي** — learners present their N2/N3/S6 pages to a partner, a group, or the wall; the course's presentational moment, powered entirely by work already done.
**C4 · Story circle · نُعِيدُ الْقِصَّةَ** — the class retells the story from the book's own panels, learner-led, teacher writing on the board only what the class says.

*The v1 teacher-engine formats are superseded: T1/T2's input duty moved to A/B; T3 (props) is struck; T4 lives on as C4, learner-led. ~~The teacher book carries C-format facilitation notes and keys~~ — the teacher book is cancelled (SC-045) and keys are not produced (SC-048); the C-format procedures live on the per-unit **teacher's page** (SC-104, §4) — and no preparation tasks, ever.*

**Classroom posters ⟨SC-114, 2026-10-10⟩.** One poster per question word (`مَنْ · أَيْنَ · هَلْ · مَاذَا · مَا …`), Arabic only, the word large over one model question quoted verbatim from a reading the class has read, with a picture in the course style. They scaffold the early exposure of question words that SC-114 licenses as support; they teach nothing. Spec: `INTALIQ1_QUESTION_WORD_POSTERS.md`.

## 5. The standard unit flow (sessions map onto phases, not the reverse)

Phase 1 at the top of class on shared devices (or B1–B2 where devices are absent) → phase 2–3 across the middle sessions with the pop-up after the main story and **the survey (C1) at the end of phase 3** → phase 4 begun in class, finishable at home (it is independent by design; the parallel story uses the survey's names) → phase 5 closing the unit — C3, C4, the personal questions, transfer — running entirely on what the unit produced ⟨SC-119⟩.

## 6. Platform engine rules (SC-028 — the online-supplement governance source per the engine spec §29)

1. Online practice is **generated from authoritative unit content** (structured unit data referencing canonical sentences, items, images, audio) — never hand-coded per unit; a correction to a reading propagates through the activity layer.
2. Input and vocabulary growth are the primary purposes; the acquisition sequence is understand → recognize → encounter again → retrieve with support → reconstruct familiar language.
3. **Language status gates behavior**: NEW_CORE_TARGET and RECYCLED_CORE_TARGET may reach supported retrieval; COMPREHENSIBLE_SUPPORT is never productively tested; RECOGNITION_ONLY is never independently generated.
4. Comprehension precedes reconstruction; meaningful chunks precede words; **Arabic letters are never scrambled**.
5. Varied contextual encounters beat repeated flashcards; recycling runs across readings and units.
6. Difficulty increases by **reducing support**, never by introducing unlicensed language.
7. **A wrong answer produces more comprehensible input** (the supporting sentence, picture, and audio replay) — never a bare ✗.
8. Automatic generation may not rewrite, re-inflect, reorder, or simplify canonical Arabic; an activity that cannot be generated correctly is **suppressed by QC**, not shown.
9. Distractors test meaning along licensed dimensions (person, object, action, possession, sequence) and come from approved unit language or authored metadata — never invented forms, never haraka-trick lookalikes.
10. **Spoiler stages gate everything**: no activity may know more than the learner has been shown; the reading unlocks stages, activities filter by them.
11. Listening activities exist **only over verified audio** (browser TTS is a preview aid, not verified audio — the listening family stays suppressed until recorded audio ships).
12. Activity quantity is subordinate to pedagogical value (eligibility model, not exhaustive generation); free production is never a platform activity (charter: output lives in the notebook).
13. Reusable components are **unit-independent** — a new unit is data, not code; this is a blocking architectural test at every unit's platform build.
14. No lives, timers, leaderboards, or point systems; one cognitive task at a time; accessible non-drag controls throughout.

16. **Learner data: completion only (SC-048).** The platform records **completion marks and nothing else** — no scores, no analytics, no behavioural telemetry, no accounts or profiles for children. This closes the privacy question open since the station spec, and it is a floor, not a default: a later feature that needs more data needs a new ruling.

15. **Items are presented inside phrases** (SC-038). Each item carries a `phrase` and an `hl`; Explore, Arabic→Picture and Picture→Arabic all render the phrase with the target marked, and audio speaks the phrase. `hl` must be a verbatim substring of `phrase` or the activity is suppressed — the canonical-integrity rule of #8, applied to carriers.


## 8. The oral gap — a tracked risk ⟨SC-093⟩

Both published CI/TPRS readers the course was calibrated against (`INTALIQ1_CI_SAMPLE_CALIBRATION.md`) are read **after** three days of a teacher telling the story with gesture and questioning. Intaliq's readings are read cold — the course has no teacher book (SC-045) and the oral phase is classroom practice, not product (SC-062). The substitutes are: the phrase page (B0), the picture strip and preview panel (B2, A5), the audio-first platform formats (A1–A4), the personal-question set (A9) and the recombination page (S10). **None has been tested with children reading alone.** ⟨**SC-124:** no longer required — no pilot is planned; see the paragraph below⟩ ~~The classroom pilot (roadmap Phase 1) must measure, at minimum:~~ The measurements once proposed: the share of a unit's readings a learner can retell from the strip without the text · comprehension on the first cold read of Story 1 versus after the phrase page and strip · whether the personal-question set is answered without the teacher · whether the recombination page is used or skipped. Until the pilot reports, every calibration tolerance is an upper bound, not a target.

⟨**SC-124 · 2026-10-13**⟩ **No classroom pilot is required or planned, and none of the measurements above is required of any unit, release, approval or completion.** The oral gap stays a named design risk, handled in design: the substitutes above, the input-before-output order (§2), repeated processing on the platform and the recombination page, scaffolded interaction prepared by modelled question-and-answer before learners use it (C1, §3), and the output ladder's planned supports. Calibration tolerances are read as design ceilings. Outward claims say learner effectiveness is untested.

⟨**WITHDRAWN — SC-124; kept as history**⟩ ~~⟨SC-122⟩~~ The four measurements above are the essential functions of Unit 1's evidence plan (`INTALIQ1_UNIT1_LEARNING_EVIDENCE_RECORD.md` §A, on the template `INTALIQ1_LEARNING_EVIDENCE_TEMPLATE.md`), observed under `INTALIQ1_UNIT1_LEARNER_CHECK_PROTOCOL.md` v2 — Check A (the cold read, preparation recorded), Check B (after the unit's actual sequence), the delayed probe. The standard (`INTALIQ1_LEARNING_EFFECTIVENESS_STANDARD.md`) fixes the vocabulary: a panel sitting is *design review*; only a learner sitting yields *observed performance*; both are recorded, neither is inferred from a build. Every later unit carries the same plan from its Gate A (template field 3b).
