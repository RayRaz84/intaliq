## 0. Entry profile and shape

**The learner arrives able to decode fully vocalized words at a deliberate pace** — letters in all positions, the three short vowels, sukūn, shadda, long vowels, tanwīn at least by recognition. Level 1 assumes **decoding only**: no vocabulary, no grammar, no fluency. (The independent script manual brings the absolute beginner exactly to this doorstep; learners who decoded elsewhere enter identically. Level 1 never presupposes the manual's content.)

Level 1 is **ten modular units** ⟨SC-063 locked the count at ten; **eight are designed** (U1–U8, this map) and **U9–U10 are locked, undesigned slots** ~~to be designed after Units 1–4 have been through a real classroom — SC-099~~ ⟨SC-124: no classroom precondition — U9–U10 are designed at their own Gate A, from the designed exit of Unit 8⟩⟩, each built around one communicative affordance, one headline grammar event, and one story environment. Units are self-contained and counted in units, never weeks. Because decoding is assumed, print and speech run together from the first lesson: every oral exchange has its readable counterpart within the same unit.

**Verification (SC-009):** the Program Owner and a panel of educators review units as they are built; U1–U2 go to the panel first, and panel findings feed forward into the later units' Gate A decisions.

Two budget principles govern throughout (Design Description §5): language is tracked in five ledgers (current core · recycled core · established support · proposed support · recognition-only), and the governing move under pressure is *redesign the situation before expanding the language*.

**Level 1 language budget ⟨SC-107, 2026-09-27⟩:** the operative band is **3–5 new-core items per unit** (Gate A template field 4; overruns disclosed, not gated, SC-090; chunks count as one). Support and recognition are uncapped and counted per unit. **No level total is claimed until measured** from the built units' ledgers. Running totals: Author's Page §9.

Grammar: **one headline structural event per unit**, minor items riding along; heavyweight machinery gets a whole unit designed around it. **Full vocalization throughout Level 1, with no taper (SC-062).** The reader series' diacritic taper — full vowelling in its first story, fewer thereafter — is **recorded as not transferring to this course**: different product, different learner, different promise. No diacritic-removal progression is authorized here. **Tense (SC-041, superseding SC-008(b); confirmed and completed by SC-073, 2026-09-15): every unit's first story is narrated in the present and its second reading in the past — so past-tense narration is present from Unit 1, not held for a late unit.** **SC-073 governs this line and resolves it against SC-062 item 1**, which had briefly held Units 2–5 to present-based narration in both stories. Three qualifications ride with it: Story 1 never uses the imperfect as pseudo-past narration; Story 2 keeps whatever nonpast forms natural Arabic requires, and is never Story 1 converted verb by verb; and **the past slot licenses no paradigm — each unit uses only the past forms licensed for that unit, verified at Gate A against the Grammar Ledger with subsection and printed page, with the unauthorized forms recorded too.** Past narration carries the past; dialogue stays present, which keeps كَانَ / لَمْ يَكُنْ out of the level (Story Standard §E-2). The pairing rule still binds wherever a *new* past structure is introduced: new structure, established vocabulary.

**Proficiency anchor (SC-010): Level 1 spans Novice Low → Novice High.** Every admission below is checked against that band and against the series: what belongs upstairs is reserved, not borrowed.

## What Level 1 refuses to spend — the deferred-constructions list (**ADOPTED**, SC-010/SC-048)

Reserved for higher levels, and therefore **never printed at Level 1, and never faked around**. A draft sentence that needs one of these is a scene problem: redesign the scene.

- **The verb system beyond the proposal above:** the full person paradigm (plural persons; أَنْتُمَا/هُمَا duals entirely); the future (سَـ/سَوْفَ); لَمْ/لَنْ negation; the jussive and subjunctive as systems; the imperative beyond fixed classroom chunks (اُنْظُرْ، اِسْمَعْ as frozen routine language only); the passive.
- **Nominal machinery (amended SC-062):** **plural formation is not licensed productively at this level in any form** — sound or broken. A plural noun may be learned **lexically, as a word**, each one an explicit ledger entry; exposure to plural words never licenses plural formation (§20L). **The dual is deferred to Level 2 entirely** — this supersedes SC-060's placement of it at Unit 5, and removes it from Intaliq 1. Also deferred: case endings as taught content (**SC-062 settles §20N's five-way distinction**: case and mood endings have **orthographic presence** and serve **reading support**; there is **no oral-realization requirement, no recognition expectation, no productive expectation and no assessment** of them anywhere in Intaliq 1 — they are printed correctly and absorbed, never explained); إضافة chains beyond two nouns; the relative clause (الَّذِي/الَّتِي); comparatives and superlatives.
- **Subordination and discourse:** everything beyond the single two-clause لِأَنَّ frame — أَنَّ-clauses after verbs of saying/knowing, عِنْدَمَا, إِذَا/لَوْ conditionals, لِكَيْ; connector chains (one connector per sentence remains the working norm).
- **Morphology as content:** roots, patterns, and derivation are never taught at Level 1 — pattern *noticing* may be seeded informally per Design Description §14, with nothing assessed and nothing productive.
- **The vocative declined:** يَا + name only, as a chunk; the يَا أَبَا construction and its family are reserved (the line's own scar).

The list is Gate A law for every unit: each unit brief must state that its story and activities were checked against it, and any needed exception comes to the owner as a recorded ruling, never as a silent workaround.

## Language control rules (**ADOPTED**, SC-012/SC-048)

**LC-1 — Admission.** A word or structure enters a unit's core only with frequency evidence and an affordance justification recorded at Gate A: it serves what the level is building, never merely what one story wants. *Redesign the situation before expanding the language* remains the standing move under pressure. **The bands are firm, not absolute (SC-046): the budget may be exceeded when a unit genuinely needs it, on one condition — every overrun is recorded where it happens, in the build doc and in the register. A silent admission is still a defect.** Product chrome and rubric vocabulary (page titles, format rubrics, bank labels, instruction imperatives) sit **outside** the envelope entirely and are not counted against any band.

**LC-2 — Accumulation.** *(Subordinate to the Vocabulary Recycling Architecture as of SC-058: LC-2 sets a percentage band for recycled running words and says nothing about **which** words return, **when**, or **whether** — which is that document's whole subject. Where they differ, V1–V22 govern.)* The envelope is cumulative and nothing expires: unit N's readings draw on all core through unit N, and every reading is **mostly old language** — 70–85% of running words recycled or transparent per teaching reading (the band both sibling programs converged on by measurement), and ≥75% with no ceiling for cumulative-type readings, whose whole job is re-encounter. Shares are measured mechanically by the established-share instrument, never by eye; an out-of-band reading returns to drafting or carries a recorded exception. ⟨**Re-aimed by CR-1 (SC-091)**: the 70–85% band is a **within-unit** measure — Story 2 against prior units + Story 1 + the phrase page, target ≥80% known by lemma, gating; Story 1 against prior units is **reported only**. The cross-unit reading of this rule was measured against two published CI readers (36–56%) and withdrawn; SC-085 dissolved.⟩

**LC-3 — Scheduled recycling.** Each unit brief names its **recycling obligations**: 5–8 specific prior items centrally re-employed in this unit's readings and activities — always including the previous two units' headline structures. Recycling is scheduled at design time, not hoped for at QC time.

**LC-4 — Grammar pop-ups.** Explicit grammar in the student book takes exactly one form: the **pop-up** — a short notice of a few lines with one example drawn from the unit's own story, appearing *after* the structure has lived in meaningful input within the unit, never before it. Noticing language, minimal terminology, no paradigm tables at Level 1. One pop-up per headline structure; minor items get none. No activity anywhere requires metalanguage to complete. Fuller explanation is the teacher book's job; the Level 1 student book carries no grammar appendix.

**LC-5 — Reading architecture (SC-013).** A unit carries at most **four** readings; three is the working norm. Fixed types: the **main story** (told orally before it is read; may run as two episodes in later units), a **parallel story** (same structures, new context and outcome — LC-2's band doing its work as narrative), and a **functional text** (roster, menu, schedule, note — a level-adapted genre text, never advertised as authentic). The **fourth slot rotates and is optional**: a culture reading, an authentic-print piece, or an extra mini-story — never more than one. Fewer readings is always permitted; more never. *A reading* is a titled passage with its own comprehension work — the ceiling cannot be evaded by relabeling a passage a "text," "challenge," or "review." **Culture pacing:** in early units culture is carried by the world of the materials (names, settings, realia, illustration) with nothing announced; Arabic culture readings (40–70 words, one focused practice, region- or context-bounded per Design Description §15) begin only when the envelope affords them — realistically the back half of Level 1. **No English culture essays anywhere in the student book.** **Authentic material at Level 1 is environmental print only** — photographed real signs, labels, packaging, with recognition tasks ("real Arabic around you") — the one authenticity claim the alignment appendix makes at Novice; authentic passages are reserved for higher levels.

**LC-6 — Length follows load (SC-017).** A reading's length is **derived from its new-vs-recycled arithmetic, never chosen freely**. Lower bound: every new core item gets **≥3 encounters in its debut reading** and **≥6 across the unit's readings** (≥8 counting activities), while new tokens stay within LC-2's share (≤25–30% of running words) — which together force a debut-heavy reading to be long enough to teach what it carries. Upper bound: **a working norm, not a wall (SC-051).** A Level 1 reading is normally **≤~120 running words** — the Novice text-type figure — and **may exceed it whenever the overrun is necessary and reasonable.** *Necessary* means the length is bought by something the reading owes: an encounter floor, a structural contrast, a craft requirement of the story itself. *Reasonable* means the reading is still recognisably the text type it claims to be — the outer test is not a number in this register but the ACTFL profile the alignment appendix stakes: a passage that grows until a Novice reader cannot finish it has stopped being a Novice text, whatever the register permits. **Two conditions survive the relaxation, both from SC-046:** the overrun is **recorded where it happens**, with a count re-run at the moment it is stated (SC-050); and it is never bought by **padding**, which LC-6 and the Story Standard both forbid and which no ruling has ever licensed. A reading that is merely long has not passed — it has skipped the test. Derived working ranges at typical loads: a main story debuting 6–10 items runs ≈80–110 words; a parallel story debuting 0–2 items runs ≈40–80; a functional text ≤30. A reading whose length and load don't reconcile is redesigned by moving items between readings, not by padding or squeezing — and, since SC-051, **squeezing is the failure mode to watch**: a text cut to hit a number it did not need to hit is as much a defect as a padded one. Unit 1 is exempt from the share measurement (there is no prior language) but not from the encounter floors. The encounter floors are heuristics adopted pending measurement; the panel's data may revise them, on the record. ⟨**Refined by CR-2 and CR-3 (SC-091)**: floors are met across Story 1 + Story 2 + the recombination page + activities, and a reading is never lengthened for a floor; length regimes are Story 1 120–250 words, Story 2 80–150, nothing under 80 is a story — the ≤~120 norm above is superseded for Story 1.⟩

## The units

Format per unit: **Affordance** (what communication becomes possible — the §2 question) · **Headline grammar** · **New-core domain** (semantic territory; exact word lists are per-unit Gate A items) · **Story environment** (Strand A, held to §6's stories-must-work-as-stories test) · **Strand tasks** (B/C/D highlights).

**U1 — This is me; who is this?**
Affordance: greeting, introducing, and identifying people — saying what you have and lack. Headline: the equational sentence as a system (pronouns, demonstratives, عِنْد-possession incl. third person and لَيْسَ negation as chunks) **plus a first limited verb set — يَقُول، يَرَى، يَدْخُل، يُحِبّ, 3sg only — enabling true narration from Unit 1, front-loaded by the in-class input station (SC-023; scoped band exception, core 23 after the لِـ family entered per SC-039)**, and the past forms دَخَلَ · قَالَ · رَأَى in the second reading per SC-041. هُنَاكَ existential. الـ admitted as glue. New core (front-loaded band): greetings chunk (السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ، أَهْلًا، مَعَ السَّلَامَة)، person words (وَلَد، بِنْت، صَدِيق/صَدِيقَة، مُعَلِّم/مُعَلِّمَة)، نَعَمْ / لَا، مَنْ؟، a first adjective pair (كَبِير/صَغِير). Story: a new student who mixes up who's who — every misidentification forces another introduction (motivated repetition, and the laugh is at the mix-up, never the student). Strands: name-chain and ball-toss interrogations (B); the class roster as the first readable real-world text (C); "This is my friend" captions (D). *First reading milestone: a learner who decoded their way in reads a page of dialogue they have already lived orally.*
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094⟩:** `قَالَ · رَأَى · دَخَلَ · أَحَبَّ` (3sg) · `أُرِيدُ` — as built.

**U2 — Where is it, and going there.** ⟨re-declared **SC-092**; previously *Where is it? The lost object*⟩
Affordance: asking and explaining location, and going to a place. Headline: **the location sentence** (definite subject + locative predicate; indefinite subject with fronted locative — Alhawary 4.2.1.3(b) p. 92, 4.2.1.4 p. 93) as the grammar target, with locatives `فِي · عَلَى · تَحْتَ` + `أَيْنَ؟` + `هُنَا` as its fillers, and the chunk **`يَذْهَبُ إِلَى ___`** (past `ذَهَبَ / ذَهَبَتْ`) as the sixth core item — band six, burden seven with `فَصْل`, one over the band and disclosed. `أَمَامَ · بِجَانِبِ` are U3 candidates (SC-084); **بِجَانِبِ is used in Unit 3 by owner direction (SC-103) — support until جَانِب's rank is read by eye, then core-eligible**; أَمَامَ's candidacy leans to U6. Support as the readings need (SC-090): `كِتَاب · بَاب · حَقِيبَة · صُورَة · بَيْت · مِفْتَاح · أَرْض · يَد`; story verbs `رَجَعَ · أَعْطَى · ضَحِكَ`; `أَيْضًا` early; singular attached pronouns input-first (off locatives); `لَيْسَتْ` input-first. Story: someone goes somewhere for something and the going has a problem — a found key whose owner is the finder; two friends crossing with the same gift. Strands: desk-map information gaps (B); a labeled floor plan (C); draw-and-caption your room (D); the recombination page and personal questions per CR-14/16.
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094⟩:** `ذَهَبَ · رَجَعَ · وَضَعَ` — go · come back · put; exposed as support: `أَعْطَى · ضَحِكَ`.

**U3 — I have, I don't have, I want.**
Affordance: possession, lack, and desire — the want-and-obstacle engine. Headline: possession completed as a system — negation (لَيْسَ عِنْدِي، with the agreement rule taught right the first time), third-person عِنْدَهُ / عِنْدَهَا, and أُرِيدُ + noun (عِنْد debuted as affirmative chunks in U1 per SC-016; U3 finishes what U1 started). New core (front-loaded): the family set (أَب، أُمّ، أَخ، أُخْت، جَدّ، جَدَّة) ⟨SC-101: the reader line's one-family-noun cap does not transfer; the set competes for the 3–5 band at Gate A; the grandparents' deferral is lifted only by a separate Gate A decision⟩، everyday objects, the hungry/thirsty pair. Story: a want blocked by lack, resolved by exchange between characters — possession change carries the emotion (§13). Strands: "find someone who has…" class searches (B); a toy-shop price sign (C); "what I have / what I want" frames (D).
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094 · SC-109⟩:** `أَعْطَى · أَخَذَ · اِحْتَاجَ إِلَى · عَاشَ` — give · take · need · live; `عَادَ` dropped from the level (duplicates `رَجَعَ`). **Built, revision 1 (SC-109):** band `أُمّ · أَب · أُخْت · أَخ · مَعَ`; «مَنْ عِنْدَهُ الْكُرَةُ؟» 158 words · «هَدِيَّةُ رَزَان» 118 words; Gate B pending.

**U4 — Yes, no, and what I like.**
Affordance: preferences and polar questions — the first opinions. Headline: هَلْ questions; **first-person** preference talk (أُحِبُّ / لَا أُحِبُّ — يُحِبّ debuted 3sg in U1 per SC-023, so U4 turns it toward the self); the لَا-vs-لَيْسَ contrast systematized (لَيْسَ عِنْدَ chunks debuted U1). New core: foods, أَيْضًا. Story: a liking-mismatch comedy — the guest who politely dislikes everything; one situational laugh, laughing at no one. Strands: class food survey (B); reading a simple menu (C); "I like / I don't like" mini-poster (D).
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094⟩:** `أَحَبَّ` (1sg productive) · `أَكَلَ · شَرِبَ · نَظَرَ إِلَى` — like · eat · drink · look at.

**U5 — Every day.** *(Structural load closed, SC-062: routines + سَوْفَ = two productive structural functions, inside §19's allowance. The dual left at SC-062; الَّذِي/الَّتِي left at SC-061.)*
Affordance: describing routines and actions happening now. Headline: the present verb, third person singular (هُوَ/هِيَ forms) plus أَنَا — deliberately not the full paradigm. New core: 8–10 high-frequency verbs (يَذْهَب، يَقْرَأ، يَكْتُب، يَأْكُل، يَشْرَب، يَلْعَب، يَسْكُن…)، كُلَّ يَوْم، time-of-day words. Story: a routine ritualized — the repetition IS the plot: a day that always goes the same way, until it doesn't (U6's hook). Strands: mime-and-say (B); a picture schedule (C); "my day" sentence chains (D).
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094⟩:** `قَرَأَ · كَتَبَ · لَعِبَ · سَكَنَ · خَرَجَ · مَشَى · عَمِلَ · بَدَأَ` — the routine set; the present-tense system taught over verbs already met.

**U6 — The search. Asking people.**
Affordance: information gaps between people — asking many people the same urgent question. Headline: the يَبْحَثُ عَنْ / يَجِدُ pair; the question cascade (أَيْنَ؟ مَنْ؟ هَلْ…؟ as chunks). New core: school-world people and places. Story: an urgent-question quest where each answer redirects the search to the next person (the event-chain engine). *(The evaluated سلوى reading is an unassigned calibration exemplar per SC-008 — a candidate for this unit if its envelope fits at Gate A, never a pre-commitment.)* Strands: hide-and-ask classroom searches (B); a lost-and-found notice (C); guided retell from pictures (D).
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094⟩:** `بَحَثَ عَنْ · وَجَدَ · سَأَلَ · عَرَفَ · سَمِعَ · أَتَى` — look for · find · ask · know · hear · come.

**U7 — Why? Because.**
Affordance: explanation and motive — reasons become speakable, feelings become explainable. Headline: لِمَاذَا / لِأَنَّ (with the single-subject rule); feeling words shown through action per §13, never announced. New core: the سَعِيد / حَزِين / خَائِف / مُتْعَب domain plus reason vocabulary. Story: a misunderstanding only an explanation can fix — the character who seems rude is actually afraid; the reveal restores dignity (the line's signature move). Strands: "why?" interview chains (B); a note explaining an absence (C); because-frames (D).
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094⟩:** `شَعَرَ بِـ · بَكَى · ضَحِكَ · فَهِمَ · فَعَلَ` — feel · cry · laugh · understand · do.

**U8 — What happened? (Past tense *completed*, not introduced — SC-041, now SC-073, puts past narration in every unit's second reading from U1. SC-073 item 4 preserves this unit's schedule in full: narrative exposure from U1 is not instruction, and U8 still owns explicit and productive past work.)**
Affordance: recounting completed events — the biggest communicative unlock in the level. **Superseded in part by SC-041:** past narration is not held for a late unit — it is in every unit's second reading from Unit 1. What U8 still owns is the *system*: past tense as productive, first-person forms, and recounting as an affordance rather than a narrative register the learner has only ever read. As proposed here: third person singular of verbs the learner already owns in the present (ذَهَبَ، وَجَدَ، قَالَ، أَكَلَ، كَتَبَ…)، أَمْسِ، ثُمَّ; the أَنَا-forms as the unit's second step — not the full paradigm. New core: minimal; this unit spends its budget on grammar (the pairing rule: new structure, established vocabulary). Story: a retelling plot — something happened while the protagonist was away, and the story is other characters recounting it, each adding a piece: motivated repetition of past forms, information assembled like a puzzle. Strands: "what did you do yesterday?" exchanges (B); a short diary entry (C); a past-tense retell of U6's story — the course retelling itself (D).
**Target verbs ⟨SC-094⟩:** `تَذَكَّرَ · صَنَعَ` — remember · make — plus the past-tense system of every verb above.

## Level 1 exit profile (what a finishing learner can do)

Reads fully vocalized connected text built from ≈120–150 owned words, alone and at growing pace. Greets, introduces self and others; asks and answers where/what/who/why/yes-no questions; states possession, lack, wants, likes; describes routines; recounts a simple completed event in the past tense. Has read at least eight real stories that worked as stories, and has used every structure in live interaction before ever being assessed on it.

The exit performance is also **Level 2's ~~empirical~~ entry profile** — ~~recorded from real pilot learners, not assumed (charter §3)~~ ⟨SC-124: Level 1's designed exit profile, revisable by design review; no pilot⟩.
