## 2. Two findings from doing this

### Flag G-1 — resolved on the record: `صَدِيقَاتٌ` is a lexical entry, not a breach

Reading 1 ends on **`وَلِي صَدِيقَاتٌ!`** — a regular sound feminine plural (Alhawary **2.3.2.2**, printed p. 53), and it is the sentence the whole story is built to reach: رزان arrives with no friend and leaves with friends. The plural *is* the payoff.

I opened this as a deferral breach and it is not one. **SC-062 §20L already rules the case**, and the wording is exact:

> *plural formation is not licensed productively at this level in any form — sound or broken. A plural noun may be learned **lexically, as a word**, each one an explicit ledger entry; exposure to plural words never licenses plural formation.*

So the rule was never "no plural words." It was "no plural *formation*, and every plural word gets a ledger entry." **`صَدِيقَاتٌ` is Intaliq 1's first such entry, and until this file existed there was nowhere to put it.** That is the ledger doing its job on its first day. The obligation it creates is narrow and now discharged: the form is licensed as itself, no other plural of `صَدِيق` is admitted, and no plural morphology is taught anywhere in the level.

### Flag G-2 — `لَيْسَ عِنْدِي قَلَمٌ` is grammatically right, and now provably so

Alhawary p. 96 carries the note that with `لَيْسَ` the predicate takes the **accusative** while the subject stays nominative — which would have made `قَلَمٌ` look wrong. The same page then gives the reversed order explicitly:

> ex. 65 — "There is not a book on the table."
> ex. 66 — "For me there is not a friend / I do not have a friend."

In the reversed order the second element — the subject — takes the **nominative**. Unit 1's `لَيْسَ عِنْدِي قَلَمٌ` and `لَيْسَ لَهَا صَدِيقٌ` are that exact construction, and example 66 is `لَيْسَ لِي صَدِيقٌ`: the same sentence the unit's main story is built on, printed in the grammar as its worked example. **The unit's central frame has a named licence at a named page.** Recorded here so no future reviewer re-opens it.

## 4. Method

Each row's page was rendered at 200 dpi with `pdftoppm` and read with `tesseract -l eng --psm 6`; the section heading and the printed running-head page number were both required to appear in the same image before the row was written. Rows whose heading did not appear on the page the OCR'd table of contents predicted were re-searched until the heading was found — this is why **2.3.2.2 is cited at p. 53** and not at the p. 50 the contents page would have suggested. The table of contents alone was **not** treated as sufficient evidence for any citation in this file.
