## 2. The apparatus chrome — separately admitted, separately governed

SC-046 admitted the can-do page's four verbs as **chrome**: language the learner meets in the book's furniture rather than its stories. They carry no encounter obligation and are never assessed.

`أَسْتَطِيعُ` · `أَتَكَلَّمُ` · `أَقْرَأُ` · `أَكْتُبُ`

Rubrics in the same class: `اِسْتَعْمِلِ النَّصَّ` · `اُكْتُبِ الْقِصَّةَ` · `قِصَّتِي الْمُوَازِيَةُ` · `دَوْرِي` · `عَرَبِيَّةٌ حَوْلَنَا` · `قَائِمَةُ الصَّفِّ` · `الْمُعَلِّمَة`.

**Flag C-1.** `أَقْرَأُ` is chrome here, while the Frequency Manual §6 allocates `يَقْرَأُ / تَقْرَأُ` (`قَرَأَ` 727 / p. 75) as **Unit 10 core** in the reader line. The first-person chrome form and the later third-person core target are the same lemma. Under form-level licensing (§1.4) that is legal — a licensed form licenses only itself — but it should be recorded now rather than discovered at Unit 10.

**Flag C-2.** The can-do page prints `هٰذَا وَلَدٌ. هٰذِهِ بِنْتٌ.` This teaches `هٰذَا / هٰذِهِ` as a frame, yet neither has an item card and neither appears in either reading except `هٰذِهِ` once in R1. **A frame taught on the last page and never met in a story is the inverse of the usual failure**, and it is worth a ruling: either card it and give it a story encounter, or move it out of the can-do list.

## 3. What the inventory found

1. **Eleven unlicensed chunks** — now licensed above, none requiring new Arabic.
2. **Four chunks carry input-first or fixed-only conditions that the platform does not currently enforce.** `لَيْسَ عِنْدِي` (B-PA5, input-first only) generates retrieval activities in the engine, because retrieval permission is read from the item's *status* and not from the chunk's condition. **This is a real breach of B-PA5 and the engine cannot see it**, because chunks have no representation in the item schema — only their heads do. Closing it means giving `chunk_ref` items their own permission row, which is a §26.7 implementation item, not a text repair.
3. **`أُرِيدُ` is the load-bearing case.** It is a chunk under B-PA6 (*never drilled as a conjugation sequence*), it is a new core target under SC-059, it occurs twice in print, and it sits below the LC-6 with-activities floor at 6. Those three facts pull in different directions and the resolution is a Program Owner decision, not an Editor's: **more encounters of the chunk** (rule-12 compliant, in dialogue) or **a recorded lower-load rationale** under Manual §2.6, which explicitly permits below-range load *with recorded rationale*.
