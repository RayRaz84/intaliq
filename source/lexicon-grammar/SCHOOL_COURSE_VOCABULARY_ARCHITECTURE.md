# Vocabulary Recycling Architecture

*Owner's document, adopted verbatim **2026-09-13 (SC-058)**. Governing, alongside the Two-Story Blueprint (SC-057), for every reading in the line. Editor's implementation notes are confined to the marked appendix.*

## Governing Principle

Vocabulary should accumulate across the course rather than disappear when a unit ends. The course must distinguish between **introducing vocabulary** and **owning vocabulary**.

A word is not considered securely available merely because it appeared several times in one lesson or one story. Vocabulary becomes useful when learners repeatedly encounter it across different days · in different stories · with different characters · in different grammatical forms · in different communicative functions · after time has passed.

> **New vocabulary should be narrow. Recycled vocabulary should be broad and cumulative.**

---

## V1 · Current vocabulary must not replace previous vocabulary

A new unit should not behave as though the previous unit's nouns have disappeared. If learners previously acquired قِطَّة · كِتَاب · مَدْرَسَة · صَدِيق, later stories should continue to make natural use of these words when appropriate. A unit may introduce a small new lexical set, but most narrative richness should increasingly come from **recombining previously known vocabulary**.

## V2 · Every unit needs a lexical ledger

*Before writing either story, create:*

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **NEW CORE** | Words intentionally introduced for acquisition in this unit |
| **RECENT RECYCLED** | Core vocabulary from the immediately preceding units |
| **OLDER RECYCLED** | Previously acquired vocabulary deliberately brought back |
| **SUPPORT** | Small amount of comprehensible language needed for naturalness |
| **RECOGNITION ONLY** | Language learners need only understand, not produce |

**Do not draft the stories until this ledger is established.**

## V3 · Story 1 carries the main lexical introduction

Story 1 should normally contain most of the unit's deliberately new vocabulary. Its lexical job is **establish → repeat → clarify → stabilize**. New core words should appear in highly comprehensible contexts and receive multiple meaningful encounters. Story 1 should combine new core vocabulary, highly familiar vocabulary from previous units, and minimal support. **Do not surround several new structures with several new nouns simultaneously.**

## V4 · Story 2 should be lexically more familiar than Story 1

Story 2 introduces a new narrative and past-time dimension. Therefore its lexical burden should normally be **lower**, not higher. Its main job is to **recombine familiar language in a new story and a new tense environment.**

Story 2 may contain a very small amount of new support when genuinely necessary, but it should rely heavily on Story 1 vocabulary, vocabulary from previous units, familiar high-frequency nouns, and previously established places, objects and relationships. This lets learners devote attention to past-tense forms, story comprehension, dialogue and narrative sequencing.

**Do not make Story 2 simultaneously introduce: a new tense system; many new nouns; many new verbs; a complex new plot.**

## V5 · New words must recur beyond the story that introduces them

A NEW CORE word should not appear heavily in Story 1 and then vanish. Where semantically natural, it should recur through several channels: oral teacher input · personalized questions · Story 1 · Story 2 · listening · dialogue · picture activities · later unit stories · functional texts · retells · cumulative review. **The goal is distributed exposure rather than local repetition.**

## V6 · Recycling should be spaced, not merely immediate

Immediate repetition is useful, but long-term acquisition requires delayed return. Planning should include **immediate** (same lesson / same story) · **short-delay** (later in the unit) · **medium-delay** (one or two units later) · **long-delay** (later cumulative stories and functional tasks).

A word should therefore sometimes disappear temporarily and then return. **This creates retrieval rather than simple short-term familiarity.**

## V7 · Not every old word must appear in every unit

Cumulative recycling does not mean stuffing every known noun into every story — that would damage naturalness, story quality and lexical focus. Select previous words because they fit the new narrative.

> **Recycle opportunistically but plan systematically.**

The curriculum must ensure long-term recurrence; the individual story must still feel natural.

## V8 · Old vocabulary should take on new jobs

The strongest recycling does not merely repeat the same sentence. Weak: *عِنْدَهُ كِتَابٌ. عِنْدَهَا كِتَابٌ. عِنْدِي كِتَابٌ.* Better recycling lets **كتاب** participate in different meanings — someone wants a book · someone has the book · a book is missing · one character gives a book · someone asks about a book · the book becomes a gift · later someone remembers or finds the book. **This develops flexible lexical knowledge.**

## V9 · Recycle words across grammatical persons

Narration: عِنْدَهُ كِتَابٌ. Dialogue: — هَلْ عِنْدَكَ كِتَابٌ؟ — نَعَمْ، عِنْدِي كِتَابٌ. Later: — مَاذَا تُرِيدِينَ؟ — أُرِيدُ الْكِتَابَ.

The noun remains familiar while the surrounding grammar expands. **Especially important for the dialogue strand.**

## V10 · Recycle vocabulary across tense

Story 1: يَذْهَبُ عُمَرُ إِلَى الْمَكْتَبَةِ. يَجِدُ كِتَابًا. Story 2, after the forms are authorized: ذَهَبَ عُمَرُ إِلَى الْمَكْتَبَةِ. وَجَدَ كِتَابًا.

**The cognitive novelty should come primarily from the tense distinction, not from an entirely new semantic world.**

## V11 · Recycle across story worlds without repeating plots

Vocabulary continuity does not require narrative continuity. The same words may participate in completely different stories — a cat wants fish · a child sees a cat at school · someone cannot find a friend's cat. قِطَّة is recycled without rereading the same cat story.

> **lexical familiarity + narrative novelty**

## V12 · High-frequency words deserve disproportionate recycling

Not all vocabulary should receive equal curricular attention. Words with high communicative usefulness should recur much more often: people · common places · common objects · high-frequency actions · question words · possession · location · wants · basic relationships.

Low-frequency support vocabulary does not need the same recycling burden. **Do not allow an incidental support noun to displace a high-frequency core word from future input.**

## V13 · Recycle verbs as meaning systems, not vocabulary items

Important verbs should recur with different subjects · different objects · different persons · affirmative and negative meaning · questions · dialogue · narration · later past forms where appropriate.

Acquisition of يُرِيدُ should eventually include meaningful encounters with يُرِيدُ · تُرِيدُ · أُرِيدُ · تُرِيدُ؟ · أَرَادَ · أَرَادَتْ, as authorized. **Do not teach these as an isolated paradigm merely to claim coverage.** They should emerge through repeated communicative use.

## V14 · Core nouns should circulate across units

Create a **cumulative noun pool.** Each unit adds only a small number of new core nouns. Later stories should draw substantially from the accumulated pool rather than creating a new thematic vocabulary list. This allows story complexity to increase while lexical novelty remains manageable.

The curriculum should increasingly produce **new combinations of old words** rather than **new words describing the same simple situations.**

## V15 · Story 2 must recycle Story 1 selectively

Story 2 should reuse enough lexical material from Story 1 to create familiarity, but not so much that it feels like the same story. Preserve key current structures, a meaningful portion of current-unit vocabulary, selected older vocabulary. Alter protagonist · narrative engine · situation · information structure · outcome.

The learner should think *"I know most of these words"* — not *"I already read this story."*

## V16 · Support vocabulary does not automatically enter the recycling pool

A support word becomes future core vocabulary only through an **explicit curriculum decision.** Do not silently promote every memorable support word. Classify after the story: **Remain support** · **Promote later** · **Recognition only**. This prevents vocabulary creep.

## V17 · Dialogue is a major recycling mechanism

Dialogue should deliberately recycle familiar lexical items through first- and second-person exchanges. A known noun such as سَمَك can appear in narration — الْقِطَّةُ تُرِيدُ سَمَكًا — and later in dialogue — هَلْ عِنْدَكَ سَمَكٌ؟ / لَا، مَا عِنْدِي سَمَكٌ.

> **person-form development + lexical recycling**

## V18 · Vocabulary recycling must cross activity types

Do not recycle a word only by repeating it in readings. A strong lexical item should reappear across processing modes: **hear it → understand it → see it → choose it → answer with it → read it → retrieve it → use it.**

Channels: teacher narration · PQA · listening · reading · picture matching · dialogue · information gaps · retelling · functional communication · writing. **This creates deeper lexical ownership than repeated reading alone.**

## V19 · Later units should become lexically easier in one sense

As the cumulative bank grows, a larger proportion of each new story should be made from already familiar language. Story complexity can increase while the percentage of unfamiliar vocabulary remains controlled. **Progress should increasingly come from richer combinations, grammar, discourse and narrative rather than simply adding more nouns.**

## V20 · Avoid thematic vocabulary dumping

Do not decide *"this is the restaurant unit, therefore students need fifteen food words."* Instead ask *"which two or three new words does this communicative situation genuinely require?"* **Themes provide contexts. They do not authorize vocabulary explosions.**

## V21 · Cumulative Recycling Map

Maintain a curriculum-level map:

| Word | Introduced | Same-unit recycling | Next-unit return | Later return | Productive? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| قطة | U1 | S1/S2 | U2 | U4/U7 | Yes |
| كتاب | U1 | S1/S2 | U2 | U3/U5 | Yes |
| صديق | U1 | S1 | U2 | U4 | Yes |
| مكتبة | U1 | S2 | U3 | U6 | Yes |

The exact recurrence points should follow natural story opportunities. **The map exists to prevent accidental vocabulary disappearance. Do not distort a story merely to satisfy the table.**

## V22 · Vocabulary Audit Before Story Approval

**New vocabulary** — How many items are genuinely new? Are all of them necessary? Could an existing known word do the job?

**Recycling** — Which current-unit words recur? Which recent words return? Which older words return? Are they being used in new combinations?

**Distribution** — Does Story 2 recycle Story 1 vocabulary? Will important new vocabulary return later in the unit? Is delayed recycling planned?

**Naturalness** — Was any old vocabulary forced into the story unnaturally? Was any natural expression rejected merely because it required one transparent support word?

**Acquisition** — Are high-value words receiving disproportionately more exposure? Are learners encountering them across persons, contexts and eventually tenses?

If recycling is only local and immediate: **`FAIL — INSUFFICIENT CUMULATIVE RECYCLING`**

## Additions to the Story 1 Design Card

**New core vocabulary** · **Recent recycled vocabulary** · **Older recycled vocabulary** · **Support vocabulary** · **Words deliberately returning after a delay** · **How current vocabulary is used differently from previous appearances** · **Planned later recycling**

## Additions to the Story 2 Design Card

**Story 1 vocabulary intentionally recycled** · **Older vocabulary intentionally recycled** · **New vocabulary, if any** · **Why any new lexical item is necessary** · **Vocabulary now encountered in past-time narration** · **Vocabulary recycled through first-/second-person dialogue** · **Planned delayed return**

## Addition to the Final Quality Gate

A story pair cannot receive final approval unless:

1. the current unit's important vocabulary receives sufficient meaningful exposure;
2. Story 2 substantially recycles rather than replaces Story 1 vocabulary;
3. previous-unit vocabulary reappears where naturally useful;
4. some older vocabulary is deliberately retrieved;
5. support vocabulary remains controlled;
6. vocabulary is recycled across more than one type of activity;
7. lexical reuse occurs in new semantic or grammatical combinations;
8. the curriculum records a future return for important newly introduced vocabulary.

> **narrow introduction → dense meaningful use → immediate recycling → delayed retrieval → new combinations → long-term ownership**

# Appendix — Editor's implementation notes (not part of the rules)

## A · What this adds to the existing machinery

The Two-Story Blueprint (SC-057) governs **narrative**. This governs **lexis**. They interlock at three points: blueprint rule 2 (core carries the story, support makes it natural) is the same claim as **V2/V21** with a ledger attached · blueprint rule 21's four-way classification is **V2's** ledger · and the blueprint's design cards (rules 26–27) now carry this document's **seven and seven additional fields**, which are appended there rather than kept in a second place.

**LC-2 (accumulation) in the working map is now subordinate to V1–V21.** LC-2 stated a percentage band for recycled running words; it said nothing about *which* words, *when they return*, or *whether they ever return*. That is the whole subject of this document.

## B · Two new standing obligations

1. **The lexical ledger (V2) must exist before drafting.** Unit 1's was reconstructed after the fact and is recorded in the map below; every later unit builds its own first.
2. **The Cumulative Recycling Map (V21) must exist and be maintained**, as a separate instrument — `SCHOOL_COURSE_RECYCLING_MAP.md`. **Final Quality Gate condition 8 cannot be answered without it**, which means no unit can receive final approval while it is missing. This is the single largest process change in the document.

## C · The tension the Editor expects to recur, named once here

**V4 and craft quality pull against each other in Story 2.** V4 wants Story 2 lexically lighter than Story 1, because the tense system is already occupying the learner. But Story 2 is also where the blueprint's absurdity licence and comic payoff usually live, and a payoff often wants a word the unit does not yet own. Unit 1 hit this immediately: the three items that made Story 2 genuinely funnier and gave it a stated want — أُرِيدُ, حَزِين, يَتَكَلَّمُ — are also exactly what strains V4.

**The resolution is not to choose between them.** It is to introduce the word in Story 1, where V3 says new core belongs, and let Story 2 recycle it — which satisfies V3, V4, V5 and V15 at once. Recorded here as the standing first move whenever Story 2 wants a new word.
