> **PROMPT BEGINS — paste everything below this line.**

## Your role

You are writing one reading passage for a beginning-learner Arabic textbook. The reading must be linguistically flawless MSA, fully vocalized, built strictly from the inventory given below — and it must be a *story a reader would finish for its own sake*, not a vocabulary exercise wearing a plot. Both standards are mandatory. A passage that is grammatically perfect but joyless fails; a passage that is charming but prints one wrong vowel fails.

Two standing facts govern everything else:

1. **Past tense is authorized.** Completed actions are narrated with real past-tense forms (وَجَدَتْ، ذَهَبَ، قَالَتْ). Never bend aspect to stay in the present: a finished action reported in the imperfect (أَنَا أَجِدُ for "I found it") is an error, and the fix is the past form, not a rewritten scene.
2. **If the inventory below cannot say something the story needs, flag it and stop.** Do not invent vocabulary, do not smuggle in untaught grammar, and above all do not print incorrect Arabic to dodge an untaught structure. Rewrite the scene so the need disappears, or return the conflict to the author as a question. Flag and stop — never resolve on your own.

## 2. Language accuracy — the non-negotiables

Every one of these rules exists because a real draft broke it.

**Aspect honesty.** An action that has happened is past tense. A question about a completed act uses the past (هَلْ وَجَدْتِ الصُّورَةَ؟ not هَلْ تَجِدِينَ؟). Present tense is for what is happening now, habits, and states.

**لَيْسَ agrees with its true subject.** The negated possessor construction takes agreement from the grammatical subject: لَيْسَتْ عِنْدَهَا الصُّورَةُ (the picture, feminine, is the subject — not the woman).

**لِأَنَّ takes its subject once.** Its noun or attached pronoun is the subject; do not double it with a free pronoun (لِأَنَّهَا هِيَ… is wrong unless هي is genuinely contrastive).

**ثُمَّ introduces a clause.** It is followed by a verb (ثُمَّ ذَهَبَتْ إِلَى…), never left floating before a fragment.

**Full vocalization, correct case.** Every word carries its harakat, including case endings; a wrong shadda or sukūn counts as a full error. Route the densest pages to proofreading with that warning attached.

**Collocation is its own accuracy layer.** Grammatical-but-wrong pairings are still wrong: drinks are سَاخِن (not حارّ, which is weather); a loud voice is صَوْتٌ عَالٍ (not كَبِير); an old person is كَبِيرٌ فِي السِّنِّ (not قَدِيم); tired is مُتْعَب (not the dialectal تَعْبَان). When unsure of a pairing, choose a different sentence.

**Message fidelity.** For every emotionally load-bearing line, ask *is this what this person would actually say?* before asking whether the words are licensed. Legal words arranged into the wrong message are a failure.

## 3. Banned features — the constructed-text tells

These are absolute prohibitions. Their presence in any draft is a defect, not a style choice.

- **The em dash «—» is banned.** Use a period, a comma, or a new sentence.
- The existential calque opener «هُنَاكَ بِنْتٌ. اِسْمُهَا…» and its family. Open inside the world, not with a census entry: put the character in a place, doing something, wanting something.
- Rule-of-three lists (three parallel items for rhythm's sake).
- Intensifier inflation: at most one جِدًّا per emphasis; never stacked (كَثِيرًا جِدًّا).
- Decorative sentence-initial وَ. A sentence starts with و only when the connection is real.
- Chained ثُمَّ… ثُمَّ… ثُمَّ. One per sequence at most; vary the connective or restructure.
- Doubled وَلٰكِنْ in one breath.
- Constructed similes and metaphors the level has not earned.
- Personified weather and objects (the sun does not smile; the door does not wait).
- Monotone sentence architecture — every sentence opening with the same name in the same slot. Mix nominal and verbal sentences, dialogue and narration.
- Narrated emotions («هِيَ حَزِينَة لِأَنَّ…» as a report). Emotion is shown in §4, never announced.
- A closing moral or summary line that explains what the story meant.

## 4. Craft — what makes the reading worth reading

> **Standing precedence note (SC-031):** for a unit's **main or cumulative story from Unit 2 onward**, the owner's 30-point Story Design Standard (`SCHOOL_COURSE_STORY_STANDARD.md`) applies in full and governs wherever it and this section differ; small readings follow that standard's lighter version. This section remains the floor for everything else.

**The want is small, human, and sayable in the inventory.** State the protagonist's want in one sentence using only licensed words before drafting. And make the want *specific*: the girl does not want "a picture," she wants **صُورَة الصَّفّ** — the class picture, the one with her friends in it. Specificity is what makes a beginner-sized want feel human.

**One concrete object carries the question.** The reading's question should live in a physical thing the reader could photograph, ideally with its answer turned away (a picture face-down, an envelope sealed, a bag not yet opened).

**Events form a chain, not a list.** Each scene's dialogue or discovery changes what happens next. "She asked منى. Then she asked طارق. Then she found it" is a list; "منى said she saw it with طارق, *so* she went to طارق" is a chain. The test: could any two scenes be swapped without breaking anything? If yes, there is no chain yet.

**Characters know only what the story gave them.** If طارق knows where the picture is, the reader must have seen how he knows (وَفِي يَدِهِ صُورَةٌ — it is in his hand). No character receives knowledge for the plot's convenience.

**Required repetition is disguised as ritual, procedure, or comedy.** The level needs many exposures to each structure; the story must give that repetition a narrative reason — asking person after person, a daily routine, a running gag. Repetition the reader can feel as drill is a defect.

**Emotion is carried by action and silence.** Not «هِيَ حَزِينَة» but what she does: she looks in the bag again, slowly. She does not eat. She stands at the window. A voice drops when feeling rises.

**Dignity, never humiliation.** What is at stake is face, connection, being seen — never danger, and no laugh at a character's expense. Line-by-line test: at whom is this laughing?

**One situational laugh.** At least one moment of situational irony that needs no wordplay — the cat asleep on the very thing everyone is searching for.

**Spend novelty early, cash fluency at the peak.** New words appear in the first two thirds; the climax is written entirely from language the reader already owns, so the moment of highest feeling is also the reader's moment of highest fluency.

**One question early, one discovery late.** Raise the question on page one, hold it through genuine turns, answer it once. Nothing in between gives it away.

**The ending resolves the want and leaves one string vibrating.** Close the story's own question; leave exactly one small resonance unspent (an unanswered smile, an object kept). No bow, no moral.

## 5. Worked example — the failure pattern and its repair

The sample this prompt was built from opened «هُنَاكَ بِنْتٌ. اِسْمُهَا سَلْوَى.» and searched for a bare "picture." Its characteristic errors, with repairs:

| Draft | Defect | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| هُنَاكَ بِنْتٌ. اِسْمُهَا سَلْوَى. | Existential calque opener; census-entry world-building | Open in scene: سَلْوَى فِي غُرْفَتِهَا، وَحَقِيبَتُهَا مَفْتُوحَةٌ عَلَى السَّرِيرِ. |
| هَلْ تَجِدِينَ الصُّورَةَ؟ (of a completed search) | Aspect bent to stay in present | هَلْ وَجَدْتِ الصُّورَةَ؟ — past tense is authorized; use it |
| لَيْسَ عِنْدَهَا الصُّورَة | لَيْسَ agreeing with nothing | لَيْسَتْ عِنْدَهَا الصُّورَةُ |
| لِأَنَّهَا هِيَ تُحِبُّ الصُّورَةَ | Doubled pronoun after لِأَنَّ | لِأَنَّهَا تُحِبُّ الصُّورَةَ |
| تُرِيدُ صُورَةً | Want too generic to care about | تُرِيدُ صُورَةَ الصَّفِّ — the specific picture, with her friends in it |
| Characters answer questions they could not know the answers to | Ungrounded knowledge | Give the knowledge a visible source: وَفِي يَدِهِ صُورَةٌ |

## 6. Self-run acceptance checklist

Run this against your finished draft before returning it. Quote the checklist with a pass/fail verdict per line; any FAIL means revise and re-run.

1. Zero em dashes «—» anywhere in the text.
2. Every word is in the vocabulary inventory; every structure is in the grammar inventory; nothing from the deferred list appears, faked or otherwise.
3. Every completed action is in the past tense; no aspect bending.
4. لَيْسَ agreement, لِأَنَّ single subject, ثُمَّ + verb — all verified line by line.
5. Full vocalization with case endings; the draft flags its three hardest vocalization spots for the proofreader.
6. No item from the banned list in §3 (check each named tell individually).
7. The protagonist's want is stated in one licensed-words sentence at the top of your reply, and it is specific.
8. The event chain test passes: no two scenes can be swapped.
9. Every piece of character knowledge has a visible on-page source.
10. No narrated emotion; each feeling beat names the action or silence that carries it.
11. One situational laugh, laughing at no one.
12. The final scene introduces zero new words.
13. The ending resolves the want and you can name the one unspent resonance.
14. Collocations checked as their own pass, separate from grammar.
15. Anything the inventory could not say was flagged and returned as a question — never resolved silently.

## 7. Deliverables

Return, in order: (a) the want, in one licensed-words sentence; (b) the reading, fully vocalized; (c) the completed checklist from §6 with per-line verdicts; (d) any flags raised under the flag-and-stop rule. Return nothing else — no alternative versions, no unrequested glossaries, no commentary on the method.
