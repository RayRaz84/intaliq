# Sample Lesson — Script Manual, Lesson 1: «أَنَا»

*The School Course line · **reassigned by SC-007 (2026-09-05) to the independent script manual** — Level 1 now assumes decoding, and this lesson demonstrates the manual's first hour instead: the learner arrives knowing nothing, and leaves having spoken Arabic, understood a tiny story, and read three letters — including the first letters of their own name · status: SAMPLE — its envelope is subject to the manual's own Gate A · the method it demonstrates (oral before print, meaning without translation, scripted teacher moves, light student pages) is the line's method everywhere*

**What this sample demonstrates:** oral language before print (§4 of the Design Description) · meaning established by gesture, picture, and situation — never translation lists · the teacher book carrying an untrained teacher through exact lines and exact moves · the student book staying light · communication from minute one, through responses the learner can already give (pointing, yes/no, a name).

---

## Lesson at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Communicative goal** | Greet; say who I am (أَنَا اسْمِي…); answer مَنْ أَنْتَ؟ / مَنْ أَنْتِ؟ |
| **Oral language** | السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ · أَهْلًا · أَنَا · اسْمِي / اسْمُكَ / اسْمُكِ · نَعَمْ / لَا · مَنْ؟ |
| **Letters** | ا · ب · م (with fatḥa, kasra, ḍamma on ب and م) |
| **Learners read** | بَ بِ بُ · مَ مِ مُ · بَا · مَا · **بَابَا · مَامَا** · the first letters of their own name |
| **Materials** | Two hand puppets or two large character cards (a boy and a girl) · name cards prepared in Arabic, one per learner · letter cards for ا ب م · soft ball |
| **Time** | One class session, ~60–90 minutes; the blocks below stretch or compress — cut Block 4 first if short |
| **Assessment** | Observation only. Nothing is graded in this lesson, and nothing is repeated aloud on command. |

**The one rule of the hour (teacher):** never ask for output the input hasn't already paid for. A learner may answer by pointing, nodding, saying نَعَمْ or لَا, or saying their own name. All of these are communication. Silence plus attention is also fine.

---

## Block 1 — The greeting ritual (10–15 min)

*The course's opening ritual; every lesson of Level 1 begins this way, so invest in it now.*

**1.** Stand at the door as learners enter. To each one, with a hand on your own chest:

> **السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ! أَهْلًا!**

Smile, gesture them in. Expect nothing back today. Some will echo it — welcome that, require nothing.

**2.** When all are seated, hand on chest, slowly, twice:

> **أَنَا… اسْمِي… [your name].**

Then an open palm toward a confident-looking learner:

> **وَأَنْتَ؟ مَنْ أَنْتَ؟** *(to a girl: **وَأَنْتِ؟ مَنْ أَنْتِ؟**)*

If they say their name — in any form, with or without أنا اسمي — beam and repeat it back inside the frame: **أَهْلًا يَا [name]!** If they freeze, answer for them warmly (**اسْمُكَ أَحْمَد! أَهْلًا يَا أَحْمَد!**) and move on. No one is cornered.

**3.** The ball game: toss the soft ball to a learner and ask **مَنْ أَنْتَ؟ / مَنْ أَنْتِ؟**. They say their name (alone is fine) and toss it onward — soon *they* are asking the question. This is Strand B in miniature: learners interrogating each other with real Arabic in the first fifteen minutes, using nothing but what the ritual just gave them.

*Teacher note — mixed ages/levels (a normal classroom): let stronger learners produce the full أَنَا اسْمِي frame and shyer ones answer with a bare name. Both are correct today.*

---

## Block 2 — The first story (10 min)

*Strand A: a teacher-told micro-drama with the two puppets. Total text: under twenty words, all from Block 1. The comedy does the teaching.*

Introduce the puppets one at a time, each "speaking" in a distinct voice:

> Puppet 1: **السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ! أَنَا اسْمِي عُمَر.**
> Puppet 2: **أَهْلًا! أَنَا اسْمِي مَرْيَم.**

Then the drama — عُمَر, very confidently, gets it wrong:

> عُمَر (pointing at مَرْيَم): **أَنْتِ… اسْمُكِ… زَيْنَب!**
> مَرْيَم: **لَا! لَا لَا لَا!**
> عُمَر: **اسْمُكِ… فَاطِمَة!**
> مَرْيَم: **لَا!**
> عُمَر (defeated): **…مَنْ أَنْتِ؟**
> مَرْيَم: **أَنَا اسْمِي مَرْيَم!**
> عُمَر: **أَهْلًا يَا مَرْيَم!**

Play it twice. Then check understanding with questions the class can already answer (point at مَرْيَم):

> **اسْمُهَا زَيْنَب؟** — class: **لَا!**
> **اسْمُهَا مَرْيَم؟** — class: **نَعَمْ!**
> *(at عُمَر)* **اسْمُهُ عُمَر؟** — **نَعَمْ!**

The class just followed a story with a problem, repetition, and a resolution — and the repetition was the joke, not a drill. (اسْمُهَا/اسْمُهُ appear here in the teacher's mouth only, as comprehension questions; they are input, not targets — the learner answers نَعَمْ/لَا.)

*If the class is loving it:* عُمَر tries the same trick on two or three real learners — and now the *learners* correct him: **لَا!** … **أَنَا اسْمِي…!**

---

## Block 3 — From sound to letter (20–25 min)

*Print begins only now — after every sound in it has lived in speech. Today: three letters, chosen because they open the course's first readable words and appear in many learners' names.*

**1. The sound first.** Say **بَ** clearly. Class hears it inside words they just used: **بَابَا**. Then show the letter card **ب**: "this is the picture of بَ." Same for **م** (from **مَامَا**, **مَرْيَم**) and **ا** (the long ā inside both).

**2. The three vowels as a family.** On the board, large:

> **بَ  بِ  بُ**

Point-and-say, with a gesture per vowel (fatḥa: hand sweeps up; kasra: hand drops down; ḍamma: fist rolls forward). Class reads the trio as you point *in varying order* — pointing games, speeding up, teacher "mistakes" for the class to catch (**لَا!**). Repeat with **مَ مِ مُ**.

**3. Long vowel joins.** **بَا** … **مَا** … then the reveal, written slowly as the class watches letters *connect*:

> **بَابَا** — **مَامَا**

Let the class read them unprompted. This is the lesson's summit: **they are reading.** Real words, meaningful since infancy, built from an hour-old alphabet.

**4. The hand teaches the eye.** Student book tracing: **ب** and **م** in isolated and connected forms, **ا**, then the two words. Teacher circulates. Right-to-left is modeled, never lectured: a dot on the right margin of each tracing line shows where the pencil starts.

*Teacher note — what NOT to do here: no alphabet song, no reciting 28 letter names, no "this letter is called bāʾ" drill. Letter names arrive naturally over the phase; sounds and words carry the load. And no writing-from-memory today — tracing only.*

---

## Block 4 — Your name in Arabic (10–15 min)

Hand each learner their prepared name card — their own name, large, fully vocalized. Each learner: finds today's three letters in their name and circles them; traces the whole name; keeps the card (it returns every lesson until they write the name unaided — that's the S1 exit performance growing week by week).

Close the loop with the ball: **مَنْ أَنْتَ؟** — but now the learner *holds up the card* while answering. Saying it, and showing it written: speech and print, introduced as the same fact about them.

---

## Block 5 — The closing ritual (5 min)

Line at the door. Each learner, on the way out, gets and gives:

> **مَعَ السَّلَامَة يَا [name]!**

*(The farewell enters as a chunk here, one lesson before the student book prints it — oral first, always.)*

---

## The student book's four pages for this lesson

Light, per the charter — the classroom engine above lives in the teacher book alone:

1. **Welcome page:** full illustration of the two characters greeting, speech bubbles with السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ / أَهْلًا — a page the learner can "read" today by memory of the scene, which is the point.
2. **The letter page:** ب م ا large; the بَ بِ بُ / مَ مِ مُ trios; tracing rows with start-dots.
3. **The first words:** بَابَا / مَامَا with family pictures; tracing; a find-and-circle (find ب in a scattered set).
4. **My name page:** a decorated frame for the traced name card work.

No translation appears anywhere; the illustrations carry all meaning.
