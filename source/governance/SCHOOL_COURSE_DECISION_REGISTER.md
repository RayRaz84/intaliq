# The School Course — Decision Register

*Append-only · opened 2026-09-04 per the charter's governance article · nothing is approved by relay*

> **What this register is (amended SC-071, 2026-09-15).** It is the **commit log**, not a source of authority. The Editorial Constitution and the external authorities **govern**; the Program Owner **decides**; this register makes a decision **operational and auditable**. The masthead formerly read *"a decision exists when it is recorded here,"* which would have made the register senior to the Constitution — a standing no ruling ever gave it. The correct formulation: **a decision is not operational until recorded here.** An owner ruling spoken and not yet recorded is a real decision that no instrument may act on; a rule in the Constitution binds whether or not this register mentions it; **and a register entry that contradicts the Constitution is a defect in the entry.***

**SC-041 · 2026-09-10 — The tense law: story 1 present, story 2 past. And absurdity is licensed.** Owner ruling, course-wide and permanent: **every unit's first (main) story is narrated in the present; every unit's second reading is narrated in the past.** Fixed slots, so the learner meets both narrative tenses in every unit, always in the same place — the contrast becomes systematic instead of accidental. Alongside it: **a reading may be absurd.** The owner's worked example is recorded as the reference shape — *a boy hunts a book through three places, fails at each, reaches the library, and the librarian turns out to be a talking cat who calmly tells him where it is while he stands there baffled* — and the pattern taken from it is **three honest failures, then a turn that is impossible rather than merely surprising, with the protagonist perplexed and the absurd party matter-of-fact.**

**⚠ This overrides a standing decision, and the override is the point of this entry.** SC-008(b) demoted past tense to *"arrives late in the level, deliberately and incrementally, after present-tense narration is owned,"* with the unit chosen at its own Gate A; the working map's deferred-constructions list carried past tense accordingly. **SC-041 supersedes both: past tense arrives in Unit 1, in the second reading, by rule.** Past tense is struck from the deferred list and the working map's "late in the level" line is retired. Recorded plainly rather than reconciled away — this is a real change to the level's grammar architecture, made by the owner with the rendered product in front of him. It is also consistent with the owner's own Story Standard criteria **21** (*"if past tense is needed, use authorized past tense"*) and **22** (*"past tense unlocks recounting"*), and with the inherited generation prompt, which has authorized past tense for textbook readings from the start.

**How the law is made affordable: narration past, dialogue present.** In a past-tense reading the narration carries the past (دَخَلَ · قَالَ · رَأَى) while **dialogue stays present**, because that is what people speak. This is ordinary narrative practice, and it is what keeps **كَانَ / لَمْ يَكُنْ out of Unit 1** — past-tense possession never has to be narrated, so no jussive and no لَمْ enter the envelope. A state that would need past narration goes into a character's mouth instead, or the sentence is redesigned. Adopted as Story Standard **§E-2**.

**Unit 1's second reading rebuilt again, to v5: «مَنْ أَنْتَ يَا قَلَم؟»** (57 running words). كريم arrives, asks the room «مَنْ عِنْدَهُ قَلَمٌ؟», and مها, زيد and فريدة each say لا — the three failures. The absurd turn costs **zero new vocabulary**: the pen introduces itself with exactly the pattern the unit has been practising, so the impossible event is carried entirely by owned language. **Grammar admitted:** دَخَلَ · قَالَ / قَالَتْ · رَأَى.

**Recorded to the Story Standard as §E** (E-1 fixed tense slots · E-2 narration past, dialogue present · E-3 the absurdity licence and its reference shape · **E-4 precedence: E-3 supersedes the generation prompt's ban on personified objects for licensed-absurd readings** — a pen may speak, though decorative personification in description stays banned · E-5 what absurdity does *not* license: no new vocabulary, no untaught grammar, no character made stupid for the joke, no discovery the reader cannot follow).
