# Charter — The School Course

*Product name: **Intaliq 1** (ruled 2026-09-13, SC-062/SC-063 — O-2 closed; instruments keep their `SCHOOL_COURSE_*` filenames by alias rather than rename, since renaming would break dozens of cross-references and buy nothing) · founding charter adopted by M. Adam Ziad, Program Owner, 2026-09-04 · companion to `SCHOOL_COURSE_DESIGN_DESCRIPTION.md`, the owner-authored governing concept, which this charter defers to on every question of method · established as a separate product by Ruling 9 of `BOOK1_GOVERNANCE_RULINGS_2026-09-04.md`*

---

## 1. Identity

A **four-skill communicative Arabic course for school-age learners**, delivered as a leveled series spanning the school years, teaching Modern Standard Arabic to learners who **can already decode fully vocalized script** — an independent companion **script manual** (a product in its own right, SC-007) brings the absolute beginner to that doorstep. Its method is the owner's governing concept in one line:

> Rich comprehensible input builds the foundation; supported interaction develops control; increasingly independent communication is the goal — and comprehensibility is the floor, not the ceiling.

Stories are a major strand and are held to the program's full narrative standard (they must work as stories), but the course is not a reader with exercises: its four strands — story and input, interpersonal communication, interpretive and functional communication, presentational production — reinforce one another inside one integrated system of student book, teacher guide, workbook, audio, illustration, and assessment, each doing a different job.

## 2. What it refuses to be

The line works because each product refuses to be the others. This course:

- **is not the Razanne graded readers** — those are private, self-paced reading experiences with no syllabus, no teacher, and no activities; this is a taught course with all four. The readers remain its natural extensive-reading companions, never its competitors.
- **is not Book 1 of the CI textbook corpus** (the `00–05` manuals' program). That is a distinct product with its own governance, its own architecture, and its own register; nothing transfers between them except by explicit, recorded import (Ruling 9).
- **is not the conventional textbook sequence** — vocabulary → grammar explanation → model dialogue → pair practice → writing exercise — whatever its covers end up looking like.
- **is not story → story → story.** Stories build the language; communication is the objective (Design Description §19).

## 3. Audience: the whole school span, leveled — one level at a time

The course is a **multi-level series**, each level targeting a grade band across the school years, with a **college edition planned later** as a derivative product. But the charter adopts the line's hardest-won planning rule: **a later level's entry profile is an empirical fact that earlier levels' real students will reveal, not a spec writable today.** Accordingly:

- Only the **first level** is designed now. Its grade band is the first open decision (O-1). Each level carries a **proficiency anchor** in its level spec; Level 1's is **Novice Low → Novice High** (SC-010), and its selections are made with the full series in mind — higher-band machinery is reserved for the level that will teach it properly, never borrowed early.
- The ladder above it is named in principle and left unspecified in detail; each level's spec is written when the level below has met real learners.
- Nothing in Level 1 may be built in a way that silently commits the upper levels (no series-wide vocabulary promises, no cross-level plot dependencies) until those levels have specs.

## 4. The design floor: the weekend and community school

The course is **engineered to survive the most constrained realistic classroom** — low weekly contact hours, mixed proficiency, and a teacher who may be an untrained native-speaker volunteer — and is thereby serviceable, automatically, in every better-resourced setting. It is designed floor-first *because of* that flexibility, and the floor is **internal design doctrine only** (SC-006): no learner-facing, teacher-facing, or market-facing text names weekend or community schools as the target. Outwardly the course is simply *for classrooms*, with flexible, modular pacing for any schedule. Binding consequences:

- **Modular pacing.** Units are self-contained and counted in units, never weeks. Any contact-hours budget maps onto the sequence; a full-time school simply covers more units per term.
- ~~**The teacher book carries the untrained teacher.**~~ **Superseded: there is no teacher book (SC-045, reaffirmed SC-062).** Each classroom format instead carries its whole instruction as one Arabic line on the page it already uses (SC-049), and the course ships with no teacher book, no answer keys, and no separate facilitation layer. **The oral acquisition and interaction engine is classroom practice the course assumes, not pages the course prints (SC-062 §4)** — it binds the author's design, which must leave room for it, rather than the product's contents.
- **Content is culturally warm and universal in its core**; setting-specific material (including any religious-context supplements) ships as optional add-ons, never load-bearing.
- **Rigor paperwork is paperwork.** Standards alignment and assessment documentation for committee-adopting schools are appendices; they change no lesson.
- **Contact hours are implementation guidance, not a book-level requirement (SC-063).** The former 75-hour floor is withdrawn. Pacing is **proficiency-oriented and calendar-flexible**: Intaliq 1's ten units may be completed in one academic year or spread over longer, and no unit's design is validated or invalidated by an hours figure. O-4 is closed in this form.

## 5. Entry point and script

**Decoding assumed; the script taught elsewhere (SC-007).** Level 1 is for students who can already decode the script and use harakat — reading fully vocalized words at a deliberate pace. It assumes **decoding only**: no vocabulary, no grammar. The alphabet is taught by an **independent script manual**, a separate product with its own narrow promise (script → confident decoding, nothing else); the manual may build its decoding practice from the course's early lexicon so that finishing it means arriving at Level 1 warmed up, but Level 1 never presupposes the manual — any learner who decodes, however they learned, enters at page one. Vocalization in the course is full at the early levels; any tapering schedule belongs to the level specifications, decided per level, never improvised per unit.

## 6. Language policy

- **MSA**, per the program standard; the course presents Arabic as a language of diverse communities (Design Description §15), and its formal stance on variety exposure is an open decision (O-3).
- **Grammar as affordance.** Structures enter when they expand what learners can understand and communicate, one system at a time, established before assessed (§10). **Past tense is authorized** for deliberate, incremental introduction — never forced early, and never faked by bending the imperfect (the aspect-honesty rule).
- **The language envelope is law.** Every unit tracks current core, recycled core, established support, proposed support, and recognition-only language; the governing move under pressure is *redesign the situation before expanding the language* — but control never licenses unnatural Arabic (§5).
- **Natural register is a separate, gated quality standard** from grammatical correctness (§9).

## 8. Governance

- **Own decision register, opened on day one**, append-only, numbered in its own series. This charter and the Design Description are its first two entries by reference. Nothing is approved by relay; a decision exists when it is recorded (Ruling 1's method). Build tooling never emits approval banners (Ruling 4). No approval is recorded against an unversioned working state (Ruling 5).
- **Gates.** The four-gate structure (concept → design → draft → release) is adopted in principle; the course's gate definitions are written with the Level 1 spec.
- **Verification is by expert panel (SC-009).** The Program Owner and a panel of educators verify the product; no classroom pilot is required. The recorded residual: panel review verifies the design, and the materials' behavior in untrained hands in live classrooms remains a known-open question until real classrooms use the product.

## 11. The first mistakes to avoid

Named here so they are cheap to refuse later: designing upper levels before Level 1 has students (the overclaim pattern two projects have beaten out of this line) · adopting style rules mid-production instead of at Gate A · letting the story strand's success justify skipping the interaction strands, or vice versa (§19's two failure modes) · letting "sold setting-neutral" drift into "designed setting-neutral" · and treating the classroom pilot as a launch formality rather than the test of the course's riskiest assumption.
