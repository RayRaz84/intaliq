## 6.2 What the reported number means

A **frequency rank, 1–5,000**, in the main frequency index — position in a 30-million-word corpus of MSA and several spoken dialects. **Rank 1 is the commonest word.** It is not a raw count and not a percentage.

Each entry also carries two trailing figures — a genre-dispersion percentage and a raw corpus count. **These are not the rank**, and they are the single largest source of false positives, because a dispersion figure of `1021` looks identical to rank 1021 in extracted text.

## 6.3 Lookup and normalization rules

1. **Three indexes exist and they corroborate each other**: the **main frequency index** (the primary evidence), the **alphabetical index arranged by Arabic roots**, and the **rank-ordered list**. A rank is accepted when it appears in entry-shaped context in the main index; agreement from a second index raises confidence but is not a substitute.
2. **Strip diacritics before matching; restore them when recording.** The extraction is unvocalized and RTL-scrambled.
3. **A candidate is not a finding until its surrounding context is read by eye** and confirms an entry — the lemma adjacent to its rank, followed by a part-of-speech tag and gloss. Numeric proximity alone proves nothing.
4. **The printed page is derived from running heads**, not from the entry: `Frequency index NN` on rectos, `NN A Frequency Dictionary of Arabic` on versos. 455 such markers exist. The page is the one whose head most recently precedes the entry's offset. **Entries near a page boundary must be checked against the next head**, because this is where off-by-one errors enter — see §6.5.
5. **A multiword frame is never a ranked item** (Manual §4.3). Rank attaches to the lexical head; the frame is a chunk.
6. **An inflected form is labelled FSC, not DFR** (§4.5). `أُرِيدُ` is an inflected form of `أَرَادَ` 126.
7. **No rank may be asserted that is not recorded in bank §2** (§4.9 rule 1) — see **Flag V-1** in the Vocabulary Ledger, where this rule and `FREQ_VERIFICATION_L1_FOUNDATION`'s independent pass are in unresolved tension.

## 6.4 Homographs and spelling variants

**Separate lemmas are separated explicitly and the distinction is recorded in the row:** `ثُمَّ` conj. 108 vs `ثَمَّ` adv. "there" 1310 · `وَجَدَ` 139 vs `وَجْد` n. 4933 · `سَمِعَ` 154 vs `سَمْع` 3194 · `أَخَذَ` 198 vs `أَخْذ` 1785 · `عَرَفَ` I 49 vs `عَرَّفَ` II 4513 · `جَدّ` 1226 vs `جِدّ` 171.

**A related noun is never evidence for a verb** (§4.4b): `بَحَثَ` 676 is the evidence for `يَبْحَثُ عَنْ`; the noun `بَحْث` 332 is corroborating only.

**The dictionary's own lemmatization is followed, never overridden** (§4.4d): `أَيْضًا` is ranked under the headword `أَيْض` 146.

**Dialect equivalents are named and excluded as separate lemmas**, and sub-standard spellings flagged (`لاكن` for `لٰكِنْ`).

**The substring trap, named.** Arabic orthography makes short function words substrings of longer content words. Confirmed collisions from the rejected extraction pass: **`قَالَ` matched inside `بُرْتُقَال`** · **`مَنْ` matched inside `مُنَى`** · **`لِـ` matched inside `أَوَّل`**. This is why rule 3 exists.

## 7.3 How trial decisions are logged

**Unchanged from current practice, which is the point.** A decision exists when the owner makes it and becomes **operational when recorded in the register** (SC-071). During the trial that means:

- Anything requiring a licensing, band, status or craft judgement stops and goes to you. No Editor resolution of an authorization question.
- Mechanical findings — a count, a form that is not printed, a citation, a drift — are executed and recorded, as they were at SC-064 through SC-069.
- Each register entry names **what it cascaded to**, so the trial's own changes are auditable in the same way the reconciliation was.
- **Anything the trial reveals about the instruments themselves** — a rule that could not be followed, a field nobody used, a conflict that only appears under generation — is recorded as a numbered finding against the instrument, not silently worked around. This is the trial's real output.

## 7.4 When the runbook governs Unit 3

**Not when it reads coherently.** Your own test: *another competent writer, or a fresh model session, produces Unit 3 from the runbook without reconstructing hidden decisions from conversation.*

Concretely, three conditions:

1. **Unit 2 is complete and through its quality gate**, so the runbook describes a path that terminated.
2. **The runbook is dry-run on Unit 3's opening steps by a session with no access to this conversation** — Gate A brief drafted from the runbook alone. Every question that session has to ask is a hole in the runbook, and the count of such questions is the measurement.
3. **The instrument findings from §7.3 are closed or explicitly deferred.** A runbook that routes around a known-broken instrument is a runbook that teaches the workaround.

Until all three hold, the runbook is a **draft record of one unit's construction** and Unit 3 is built the same way Unit 2 was — with the runbook open beside it, being corrected.
