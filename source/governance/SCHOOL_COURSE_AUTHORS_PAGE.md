## 2 · How a reading is written

1. **Situation first, in one sentence, with no Arabic.** A small human want, a concrete object that matters, a person who is wrong once, an ending that is earned or unexpected. Write it down before any Arabic exists.
2. **Design card** (Blueprint 26 or 27), including the fields SC-091 added: the absurd element and what it does (CR-9) · story verbs used (CR-6) · cast for attribution (CR-12) · who is introduced and with what detail (CR-10).
3. **Unit verbs first (CR-4).** Try every line with the verbs and frames the course already has before a support word enters it.
4. **Furnish from the envelope**, then from support under §4. Every support item is listed on the phrase page (CR-13) and in the disclosure table.
5. **Length by slot (CR-3):** Story 1 120–250 words, each core structure ≥8 times across polarity and person; Story 2 80–150, turn-driven; nothing under 80 is a story.
6. **One earned emotion word at most (CR-8); one absurd element per unit pair (CR-9); retellings go to the apparatus, never Story 2 (CR-11).**
7. **Title it as a storybook would** (SC-098): the story's question or its central object and people — «أَيْنَ مِفْتَاحِي؟», «صُورَةُ رَزَان وَخَدِيجَة» — never the grammar target or a vocabulary item; proposed on the design card, confirmed at Gate B.
8. **Audit** against Blueprint 28, the Standard's numbered criteria, CR-5 if the surface is a search, the antecedent check for pronouns, the dignity check line by line.

## 3 · Slots and tense (SC-073)

Story 1 is narrated in the present; Story 2 in the past, dialogue present, and is a **different story** (Blueprint 22: three substantive differences beyond tense, SC-074). Past forms are licensed **per form** at Gate A with Alhawary subsection and printed page; from Unit 2 they are `دَخَلَ · قَالَ · رَأَى · ذَهَبَ · رَجَعَ · وَضَعَ` with their feminines (SC-092, SC-094). **Past narration never drifts into the present outside dialogue** (SC-095): a state that would need `كَانَ / لَمْ يَكُنْ` goes into a character's mouth, or the sentence is redesigned; nominal descriptions («رَزَان حَزِينَةٌ») are not tense errors.

## 3b · Verbs (SC-094)

Every unit carries **3–6 target verbs** on their own track (`SCHOOL_COURSE_L1_VERB_INVENTORY.md`), productive in their home unit with past 3sg forms licensed there; a verb may be exposed earlier as a story verb in support (CR-6). **First-person forms are licensed in dialogue and in the personal-question set from the unit of first exposure.** Unit 2: `ذَهَبَ · رَجَعَ · وَضَعَ`.

## 4 · What "support" means (SC-090)

A blocked **word** enters as `support`; a blocked **form or structure** as `G-input_first` (or `G-orthographic_presence` if purely orthographic). In every case: printed where the reading needs it · never explained on the page · never assessed · never a productive target · listed in the ledger with real evidence or marked **lookup owed** · encounter-tracked · **disclosed in the drafts file's table before Gate B.** Blueprint 21 still binds: if removing the support makes the story unintelligible, the load is too high. The unit the scope map assigns an item to still makes it productive. Up to **three story verbs per unit** may enter this way (CR-6); attached pronouns singular only and off locatives until Unit 3 taught them; `لِأَنَّ` in dialogue once per reading (CR-7).

## 5 · Measurement (CR-1, CR-2)

**Gates:** Story 2 against *prior units + Story 1 + the phrase page* at **≥80% known by lemma** (names excluded, clitics in host, inflected forms of known lemmas count). Encounter floors (≥3 debut · ≥8 with apparatus) across both stories, the recombination page and activities. `canonCheck` and the form guard.
**Reports only:** Story 1 against prior units · the new-core band (3–5; overruns disclosed) · per-unit support count · owed-lookup count · name density · running words per slot.

## 6 · Cast and world

No standing cast (SC-089). **رَزَان is the single recurring figure** (SC-079, confirmed SC-092); everyone else is new to their unit and introduced on the page with one picturable detail (CR-10), described no further than the envelope allows. Settings are open (SC-087); a setting costs only the words the story prints. Drawing style is the house style (Cast Bible §2, still in force). Pair characters for attribution where the sensitivity law allows (CR-12).

## 7 · The apparatus every unit ships (CR-13 – CR-16)

The **phrase page** (numbered picture-phrases for every new item the readings print; a reading aid, never assessable by listing) · the **picture strip** before the text (meaning first; no frame reveals what the text withholds) · the readings · the Unit-1 formats (read-match, true/false with pointing, sequence, who-says, choose-and-write, text hunts) · **short answers with "if the answer is not in the story, invent it"** · **Version A / Version B** picture-only retells · the **recombination page** (8–12 one-breath texts, headed *read, then read again*) · the **cumulative personal-question set** in the preview activities · the unit's **grammar pop-up** (LC-4: one per unit, no grammatical term; the book's only English — Unit 2's is owed at Gate B) · the three learner-facing **C rubrics** printed where they happen (SC-049). Outside the products, at freeze, the unit also generates its **teacher's page** (SC-104: unit flow, C1/C3/C4 procedures, pilot prompts; no keys; never linked from the Studio).

## 8 · The scope map is an assessment map (SC-093)

`SCHOOL_COURSE_L1_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md` says what each unit **assesses** — its headline, its core, its bands. Readings **expose** what they need under §4. Unit 2's headline is *Where is it, and going there* (SC-092).
