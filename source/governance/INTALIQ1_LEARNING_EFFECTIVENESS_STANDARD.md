# INTALIQ 1 Learning Effectiveness Standard

Version: proposal 1.0, 12 October 2026. Owner: instructional Text role. **Status: ADOPTED 2026-10-13 — SC-122; SUPERSEDED IN PART 2026-10-13 — SC-124** ⟨marks added by Intaliq Text; text otherwise verbatim⟩.

> ⟨**SC-124 — what still governs, and what is history.** The owner cancelled every pilot, learner trial, observation study, feedback survey, retention or transfer study, and every empirical evidence record, as a requirement to produce, approve, complete or publish Intaliq. **§§3–7 and §9 no longer govern** (evidence plans, observation, evidence collection, review decisions on observations, build checks of evidence records) and are kept as history; §8's release and completion paragraphs and §10's observer duties lapse with them. **Still in force:** (i) **the claim limits** — outward wording names only the evidence that exists (today: technical verification; design review once recorded under SC-009), states learner effectiveness as untested, and never presents a build, a count, a panel review or the absence of evaluation as evidence of learning (§2's categories as claim vocabulary; §8's last paragraph); (ii) comprehension, retention and transfer remain **instructional aims**, pursued through the quality controls the Author's Page lists — no study is required to pursue them; (iii) §10's **privacy floor** (SC-048). No evaluation requirement replaces what was cancelled.⟩ Applies to all ten units when adopted. Existing language authorizations, frozen canons and output rungs remain governing.

## 1. Purpose and authority

This standard makes instructional decisions answerable to evidence of what learners understand and can do. Governance cannot guarantee learning. It can require suitable evidence, expose weak assumptions and prevent claims that exceed the evidence.

Existing anchors:

- Charter §§1, 5–6: four skills; decoding assumed, vocabulary not assumed; comprehensibility and natural Arabic.
- ACTFL Alignment §§1 and 5: claims concern performance within taught domains; modes are separate; learner evidence supersedes design estimates.
- Activity Architecture §§2, 6 and 8: acquisition sequence, language-status permissions, independence conditions and the untested cold-reading assumption.
- Output Ladder SC-096: production follows the unit's rung; scaffold fading is revisable.
- SC-119: C1 precedes writing that depends on it.
- SC-121: Unit 1 remains a review build until its stated completion conditions are met.

This proposal adds an evidence standard. It does not import the reader line's tense rules, introduce new vocabulary, authorize a teaching method change or replace an existing gate. Conflicts go to the governing project register before adoption.

## 2. Evidence categories

Report these separately. They are not interchangeable approval levels.

| Category | Evidence | Claim it can support |
|---|---|---|
| Technical verification | Build, references, assets, paths and browser checks | The specified functions run in the tested build |
| Design review | Educators inspect Arabic, sequence, supports and task demands | The design is judged suitable, with named conditions and unresolved risks |
| Observed supported performance | Learners perform after documented teaching with named supports | These learners completed these functions under these conditions |
| Delayed performance | Learners perform later; elapsed time and intervening exposure recorded | These learners retained access to this language over this interval |
| Transfer | Learners use authorized language with changed names, objects, roles or context | These learners reused familiar language beyond the exact practiced item |

A classroom observation without a comparison condition cannot establish that this course caused an improvement or outperforms another method. A small convenience group cannot establish a population success rate. No category establishes general proficiency or guarantees acquisition.

## 3. Planning before a unit is built ⟨SUPERSEDED — SC-124; not a requirement⟩

Each unit must carry an evidence plan beside its Gate A brief. Reuse the evidence template; do not create a separate assessment product for learners.

Record:

1. The communicative function and the current output rung.
2. What each skill/mode is expected to permit at this unit, including intentionally deferred production.
3. What prior language the function needs; where the new language first becomes comprehensible; where it is processed again.
4. The supports the learner may use for each task: pictures, canon, bank, models, replay, peer rehearsal or teacher cue.
5. The later task that reuses the language, and the later unit responsible for its delayed return.
6. The riskiest assumption and a probe that can reveal its failure.
7. The build/version and authorized language envelope used for the probe.

The plan must follow input → repeated processing → scaffolded interaction → limited meaningful output → transfer. It must not require every word or structure to become productive in its debut unit.

## 4. What must be observed ⟨SUPERSEDED — SC-124; not a requirement⟩

| Dimension | Suitable observation | What is insufficient |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning from reading | Identify participants, possession, an intention or an event; locate textual support | Reading aloud accurately or recognizing the same answer layout |
| Meaning from listening | Respond to a verified clip before its transcript is shown; disclose picture support and replay | Choosing an answer while reading the prompt transcript |
| Repeated processing | Track whether learners revisit meaning and can handle another licensed occurrence | Generated activity totals or number of clicks |
| Interpersonal exchange | A partner asks and receives an answer that matters to the grid or next task | Reciting both sides of a model without attending to the partner |
| Supported speaking | A brief meaningful response or caption with the supports permitted at the rung | Requiring a fluent free retell in Unit 1 |
| Supported writing | Appropriate meaning and referents in framed captions or a vignette | Copying a bank word into a slot without understanding its relation |
| Independence | Complete the task using allowed resources; record every extra intervention | Completion achieved after an adult supplies the answer |
| Retention | A short later probe before additional review, with intervening exposure recorded | Repeating the same item immediately after feedback |
| Transfer | A small changed situation using taught language and rung-appropriate support | An unfamiliar topic or an unlicensed form disguised as transfer |
| Engagement and feasibility | Notice whether learners follow the plot, return to the text, persist or disengage; record time and device constraints | Treating smiles or a preference vote as proof of learning |

Comprehension may be shown through pointing, ordering, choosing or another low-output response. An observer may use a neutral clarification outside the Arabic task to distinguish misunderstanding the instructions from misunderstanding the language; record that intervention. Such a diagnostic exchange is not new learner-facing course content.

## 5. Support and assistance recording ⟨SUPERSEDED — SC-124⟩

Use these labels in observer records. They are descriptions, not learner grades.

- **Within planned support:** only resources and cues explicitly allowed by the task/rung were used.
- **Extra assistance:** an unplanned cue, translation, reformulation or adult intervention was needed.
- **Answer supplied:** a person or visible key effectively gave the response.
- **Not observed:** the learner was absent, the task was not reached, an asset was missing or another condition prevented the observation.

A replay or printed bank is not a failure of independence when that task permits it. A planned teacher cue for Unit 1 C4 is not an unplanned intervention. Record both initial response and any supported recovery. Identify the reason for difficulty: decoding, language meaning, instructions, task design, technology, attention or unknown. Do not automatically attribute every difficulty to vocabulary load.

## 6. Evidence collection ⟨SUPERSEDED — SC-124; no collection is required⟩

Use the intended learners and record the actual entry profile. Do not assume that a child who can decode has zero previous Arabic vocabulary; document previous exposure. Record ages/grade band, classroom setting, teaching exposure, elapsed time and supports without identities in the shared record.

A first small trial can use the existing Unit 1 protocol's three to six learners. That number is a feasible diagnostic starting point, not a validated sample-size requirement or evidence of general effectiveness. Broaden observations if the claimed audience includes materially different entry profiles or classroom conditions.

Observe learners after the teaching that the unit actually promises. A deliberately restricted preparation trial may diagnose the cold-read assumption, but label it restricted; it cannot stand in for performance after the full unit sequence.

Use a short later probe at the next available lesson, ideally within 3–7 days for the first local trial. This interval is proposed implementation guidance, not a research-derived acquisition threshold; calendar-flexible pacing remains governing. Record the actual interval and exposure rather than forcing a date. A revised version needs fresh evidence for the function materially affected by the revision.

## 7. Review decisions ⟨SUPERSEDED — SC-124⟩

Before observation, name the essential functions, permitted supports and interpretation rules. Any numerical benchmark must be identified as a local diagnostic rule with a rationale; no universal percentage pass mark is created by this standard. The old Unit 1 3/4 and 3/5 counts may be retained as descriptive indicators, but do not by themselves establish learning or close the evidence record.

After observation, decide per function:

- **Supported by the observation:** explain what learners did and under which supports; give counts and denominator where helpful.
- **Revise and recheck:** the task/resources obstruct meaning, or intended learners repeatedly need help beyond the specified supports.
- **Insufficient evidence:** exposure, assets, attendance or administration prevent a defensible interpretation.

Never average away a recurring failure in an essential function. A single difficulty is recorded and investigated; it does not automatically invalidate the unit. A task that has multiple defensible answers, a missing resource or unearned language is a material defect regardless of the cohort's aggregate performance.

For each revision, record the observed difficulty, proposed explanation, source change, expected behavioral improvement and recheck result. Explanations remain hypotheses until tested. Prefer repairing input, response resources or scaffolding before expanding language or rewriting a frozen story. Reopening canon or changing the architecture still requires the existing owner process.

## 8. Release, completion and claims ⟨in part — SC-124: the claim rule (last paragraph) stands; no evidence status gates release or completion⟩

Technical release and empirical evidence status must be separate fields. A review build may advance while learner evidence is pending, subject to its existing technical and content gates. This standard does not silently repeal Charter §8/SC-009's expert-panel release route.

Panel-only evidence can support design review; record learner performance, retention and transfer as **not observed**. Panel members role-playing learners do not supply learner evidence. A Unit 1 closure decision must reconcile SC-121's learner-check wording with the existing protocol's panel alternative in the register.

Outward claims must name the observed mode, domain and conditions. “Instructionally observed with six learners after the stated sequence; delayed listening not observed” is more defensible than “proven effective.” An evidence ledger must never be used as a proficiency certificate.

## 9. Build and revision governance ⟨SUPERSEDED — SC-124; the build checks no evidence record⟩

The build may check that plans/records exist, version references match and status fields are populated. It must not infer teaching quality from encounter counts or write evidence results, panel approval or learner approval itself. “Not run” is a valid evidence status and must not be converted to “pass.”

All learner evidence is tied to an immutable commit or identified material version. Later changes to language, supports, modality or task sequencing trigger review of which evidence remains applicable. Cosmetic changes need not trigger repeat learning trials unless they affect legibility or response interpretation.

No new activity family is created by this standard. Probes belong to internal instructional verification; any probe added to the product must enter through the closed-catalog and language-authorization processes.

## 10. Records and responsibilities ⟨in part — SC-124: the privacy floor stands; observer and record duties lapse⟩

Text prepares the plan and revises the materials. The teacher/observer records actual delivery and responses. The panel interprets design fit and learner evidence with limits stated. The owner settles conflicts and adopts changes through the governing project register. The Publisher publishes an authorized build; publication does not alter evidence status.

Preserve SC-048's no-learner-score product policy and current privacy rules. Internal observation counts are permitted only within the existing protocol's scope; no names, accounts, public comparisons, audio/video recordings or identifiable work uploads are authorized here. Use temporary participant labels within a sitting. If linking the same learner across sittings is necessary, the teacher keeps that linkage locally under existing permission; shared records use aggregate results or de-identified excerpts only. Do not commit identifiable student data to the public repository. New retention/consent practices require owner policy rather than invention in this document.
