# The School Course — Level 1, Unit 1 · survey packet

A four-skill communicative MSA Arabic course for school-age learners (~8–13). This packet is everything an outside reviewer needs to survey Unit 1 without access to the live Studio.

## What the course is trying to be

Three products that deliberately refuse to be each other: a **student book** (input and reading), a **notebook** (output), and a **platform** (input and vocabulary practice). A fourth layer — three classroom formats — runs on pages the other products already print. There is **no teacher book and no answer key**, by ruling: every print set is self-checking against the page it sits on, and each classroom format carries its whole instruction as one Arabic line on the page it uses.

## The constraints a reviewer should judge it against

These are the rules the material is *supposed* to obey. Breaches are the useful finding.

1. **The envelope.** Unit 1 has a 23-item core. Words outside it appear only as page furniture (rubrics, headings), which is licensed but must be recorded.
2. **Length follows load.** A reading's length is derived from its encounter arithmetic, never chosen. Every new core item gets ≥3 encounters in its debut reading, ≥6 across the unit's readings, ≥8 counting activities. ~120 running words is a working norm, exceedable when necessary and reasonable — never by padding, and never squeezed to hit the number.
3. **Tense law.** The first story is always present; the second always past. Past narration with present dialogue, which is what keeps كَانَ out of Unit 1.
4. **The possessive split.** لِـ for people, عِنْدَ for things — never explained, simply always printed correctly.
5. **Phrases, never bare words.** Every prompt and option in every activity is a phrase or sentence. A bare vocabulary item is a defect.
6. **One grammar pop-up per unit**, and it is the student book's single element of English.
7. **Independence.** Could a student alone in a room finish this with only the products in front of them? Zero prep, zero props.
8. **The platform composes no Arabic.** Every string is a reference into canonical data; the engine only arranges. Failing activities are suppressed, never shown degraded.
9. **Friendship claims are same-gender only.** Nothing that could read as romance; يُحِبّ is aimed at things, never at named persons.
10. **Western digits 0–9 only.** No ٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩ anywhere.
11. **The platform records completion marks only** — no scores, no analytics, no accounts or profiles for children.

## What is known to be missing, so you needn't report it

No recorded audio (the whole listening family is suppressed by rule until real audio exists, so a four-skill course currently ships three) · no photographs for the authentic corner · no typeset print layout — the Studio is a preview, not a page design · no unit-opener art · illustrations are specified as stick figures on true white but not yet drawn.

## What a survey would most usefully attack

- **The Arabic itself**: vocalization errors, agreement, and above all **collocation** — whether a native ear would actually say these sentences. This has never had a native-ear pass.
- **The story as a story**: does it have a real want, a real event chain, a laugh, a resolution? Is the second reading genuinely a different engine from the first, or the same plot redressed?
- **Whether the activities teach or merely test**, and whether any of them can be answered without understanding the Arabic.
- **Breaches of the eleven constraints above**, with the specific line quoted.

Reviewer notes are **input, not approval**: nothing in this course is approved by relay. Findings come back as findings, and the owner rules.
