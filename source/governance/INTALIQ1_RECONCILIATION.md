# Part 1 — RULED

## R-1 · The existential opener: the prohibition is replaced, not deleted

**The conflict.** `READING_GENERATION_PROMPT.md` §3 lists among absolute prohibitions: *"The existential calque opener «هُنَاكَ بِنْتٌ. اِسْمُهَا…» and its family."* The approved Unit 1 main story opens **«هُنَاكَ بِنْتٌ اسْمُهَا رَزَان.»** — the owner's own v7 text, adopted verbatim. An instrument in force forbids the sentence the flagship product prints.

**Ruled (Program Owner, 2026-09-15).** The approved Unit 1 opener governs. The prohibition is **replaced with the principle it was reaching for**, not struck:

> **Existential openings are permitted when they are natural, narratively functional, and not used as a repetitive textbook formula.**

**Why the replacement and not the deletion.** The original prohibition was diagnosing something real — the census-entry opening that introduces a character as a database row and tells the reader nothing about what anyone wants. Unit 1's opener is not that: «هُنَاكَ بِنْتٌ اسْمُهَا رَزَان. تَدْخُلُ الْفَصْلَ. رَزَان لَيْسَ لَهَا صَدِيقٌ... وَعِنْدَهَا قَائِمَةٌ.» establishes a person, a place, a lack and an object inside four clauses, and the lack and the object are the story's engine. The failure mode is the **formula**, not the construction. Deleting the rule would have lost the diagnosis; keeping it would have condemned the product.

**The operative test for a writer.** An existential opening passes if removing it would cost the reader something they need in the first breath. It fails if it is there because the previous unit's story started that way.

**Cascade.** `READING_GENERATION_PROMPT.md` §3 amended. **But see R-2: that file has larger problems than this line** and the amendment does not make it fit for use.

## R-2 · The Decision Register records decisions; it does not create them

**The conflict.** The Register's masthead reads *"a decision exists when it is recorded here; nothing is approved by relay."* The Editor repeated that as *"the only place a decision exists."* That cannot stand beside an authority stack in which the Editorial Constitution and the external sources govern authoritatively — it would make the Register senior to the Constitution, which no ruling has ever said.

**Ruled (Program Owner, 2026-09-15).** The correct formulation:

> **A decision is not operational until recorded in the Register.**

**What changes in practice.** The Register stops being a source of authority and becomes the **commit log**: the Constitution and the external authorities *govern*; the owner *decides*; the Register makes a decision **executable and auditable**. An owner ruling spoken and not recorded is a real decision that no instrument may yet act on. A rule in the Constitution is binding whether or not the Register mentions it.

**And it removes a trap.** Under the old reading, an instrument could be "corrected" by writing a Register entry that contradicted the Constitution. Under the new one it cannot: a Register entry that conflicts with the Constitution is a defect in the entry.

**Cascade.** Register masthead amended.

## R-4 · `أُرِيدُ` sits below the LC-6 floor and must be adjudicated, not carried

**STATUS: CLOSED by SC-077, 2026-09-15 — and it dissolved rather than needing a ruling.** Counted across *all* canonical Unit 1 materials — both stories, the twelve textbook activity sets, the can-do page, the roster tasks and the platform — **every core item clears the ≥8 floor**: `أَرَادَ` 18 · `أَحَبَّ` 13 · `وَلَد` 14 · `بِنْت` 28 · `أَهْلًا` 21 · `السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ` 23. **The measurement.** `أُرِيدُ` occurs **twice in print** (once per reading) and carries **4 platform activities** — **6 encounters** against LC-6's *≥8 counting activities*, and **1 encounter in its debut reading** against *≥3 at debut*. It fails both the debut floor and the with-activities floor.

**Why it is here at all.** SC-059 moved it into Story 1 because V3 puts new core in the first reading, and the move closed V3, V4, V5, V15 and Blueprint rule 3 simultaneously. It is also a chunk under bank B-PA6 — *"meaningful chunk only, never presented, drilled, or assessed as a conjugation sequence"* — which caps how much apparatus it can carry.

**The honest options.**

**(a) Raise the encounters.** Add `أُرِيدُ ___` occurrences in dialogue, rule-12 compliant (varied surfaces, not repetition). The natural sites: رزان's search gives her a second want («أُرِيدُ صَدِيقَةً!» when she sees سارة), and the can-do page already licenses «أُرِيدُ ___» as a speakable frame. **Cost:** touches approved text.

**(b) Authorize it as an explicit exception with recorded rationale.** Bank §2.6 permits below-range load *"with recorded rationale."* The rationale would be that B-PA6 caps its apparatus, so the floor cannot be met without violating the chunk rule — the two rules are in tension and the chunk rule is senior. **Cost:** none to the text; the precedent is that LC-6 yields to B-PA6, which must then be stated as a general rule, not an ad-hoc pass.

**(c) Revise the LC-6 threshold.** The scope map already says the floors are *"heuristics adopted pending measurement; the panel's data may revise them, on the record."* **Cost:** the honest move is to revise them on panel data, not on one inconvenient item.

**(d) Replace it.** Move `أُرِيدُ` out of Unit 1 back to Unit 3, where the possession system completes. **Cost:** unwinds SC-059 and removes the want from Story 1, which is what rule 3 asked for.

**Recommendation: (b), stated as a general rule.** Six items fall below the floor (below), and five of them are chunks or formulae whose apparatus is capped by the same chunk rule. That is not six exceptions — it is one missing rule: **an item whose licensing forbids drilling cannot be held to a floor that assumes drilling.** Write that once and five of the six close.

**The other five, for the same ruling:** `السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ` 6 · `أَهْلًا` 7 · `بِنْت` 7 · `وَلَد` 5 · `أَحَبَّ` 5. Of these, **`بِنْت` and `وَلَد` are ordinary nouns and are not covered by the chunk argument** — they are simply under-encountered, and (a) is the right answer for them.

## R-8 · Unit 3's six family nouns against a cap of one — **CLOSED, SC-101 (2026-09-19): option (a) adopted; the cap does not transfer; the grandparents' deferral stands separately**

Bank §4.2 (B-PP4) caps **new family nouns at one per unit**. The scope map gives Unit 3: `أَب · أُمّ · أَخ · أُخْت · جَدّ · جَدَّة` — six.

**Options.**

**(a) The cap is a reader-line rule that does not transfer.** There is precedent decided the same way: SC-062 item 8 recorded the reader series' diacritic taper as **explicitly not transferring** — *different product, different learner, different promise.* The reader meets one family member per story across a whole volume; a classroom course teaching "my family" as a communicative affordance cannot introduce one noun per unit and take six units to do it. **Recommended.**

**(b) Split across units.** `أَب · أُمّ` in U3, `أَخ · أُخْت` in a later unit, `جَدّ · جَدَّة` later still. Faithful to the cap, and it makes "talk about your family" unavailable until Unit 8.

**Note for (a) or (c):** `جَدّ` is **1226** (HUC-V) and the bank already marks it *"DEFER unless a U7+ brief casts a grandparent."* Whichever option is chosen, the grandparents have a separate standing deferral that has to be lifted deliberately.
